DeepQuest: From Knowledge Gaps to Scientific Discovery in Alzheimer's Disease

Chunbo Jiang, Yifei Chen, Beining Wu, Fuyou Mao, Weihong Chen, Enyu Bao

Automating experiments does not necessarily advance scientific understanding. Research agents must identify meaningful knowledge gaps, determine what available data can resolve, and assess whether their findings advance existing knowledge. We introduce DeepQuest, a knowledge-gap-driven AI scientist framework for Alzheimer's disease research. DeepQuest transforms unresolved questions from domain reviews into traceable, testable hypotheses and prioritizes them through data feasibility screening and groupwise Elo ranking. It couples iterative experimentation with two-level auditing that separates experimental reliability from knowledge contribution relative to two frozen literature corpora. On OASIS-3, DeepQuest produces 42 feasible hypotheses and outperforms the strongest baselines in 14 of 15 evaluator-dimension comparisons across three language-model reviewers. The full framework achieves an audit-assessed scientific finding rate of 95.24%, outperforming both general-purpose executors and domain-specific research agents. This scientific finding rate remains stable across literature corpora and evaluator model families. Representative findings concern neuropsychiatric symptoms and dementia risk, longitudinal amyloid spatial stability, and neocortical tau-associated clinical impairment, with key associations supported in independent ADNI and AIBL cohorts. These results show how aligning knowledge gaps, data feasibility, and evidence auditing can turn automated research into traceable, evidence-supported scientific discovery. Our source code is available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/DeepQuest-3066/

Seer: Guiding Small Deep-Research Agents with Shared Decision Models

Yuhao Zhou, Yang Zhou, Yuanxi Li, Jindi Lv, Dan Si, Qing Ye

Deep-research agents repeatedly decide what to search, read, and verify, often using large cloud-hosted language models for action proposal. However, privacy and local control motivate running agents on users' devices, where limited memory and compute favor smaller models and complicate on-device training. Yet inference-time guidance faces three obstacles: exploring alternatives through real execution adds tool calls, prompted self-evaluation is bounded by the agent's judgment, and model-specific guidance risks repeated adaptation costs. We introduce Seer, an inference-time framework providing local guidance that separates the agent's action proposal from learned consequence prediction and value estimation. From a shared corpus of real research trajectories, we train two models: a tool-response simulator on observed call--response pairs and a value estimator on histories labeled with discounted terminal correctness. The simulator and estimator are trained offline and reused across agents without further training. Moreover, Seer executes only the selected action and adds only real tool responses to the committed history, avoiding tool calls on discarded candidates at additional inference cost. Across multiple deep-research benchmarks, Seer substantially improves agent performance for different language models. For instance, GAIA accuracy rises from 13.6% to 41.7% for Qwen3.5-4B and from 22.3% to 50.5% for Qwen3.5-9B. Furthermore, our analyses suggest that broader candidate coverage can compensate for imperfect simulation, while prediction and scoring errors limit the benefits of wider imagined search. These findings highlight the potential of reusable models for prediction and evaluation to strengthen small research agents through extra local computation without the real execution of the discarded candidates.

How to Effectively Manage the Energy in Residential Systems: Development and Comparison of Methods

Julian Ruddick

The transition from fossil fuels presents significant challenges in energy management. To ensure a sustainable energy future, it is crucial to reduce energy consumption when possible and to synchronize electricity consumption and storage with the production from sources with low greenhouse gas emissions. This thesis explores a variety of energy management methods used to manage batteries, heat pumps, and other energy-related systems. The thesis presents three simulation cases alongside one real-world experiment to compare energy management done through simple rules, optimization, and machine learning methods. Furthermore, this thesis introduces TreeC, a novel machine learning-based method that learns understandable yet efficient energy management strategies, applicable to many cases. The three key contributions of this thesis can be summarized as follows: 1) The developed TreeC method demonstrates competitive performance compared to a variety of energy management approaches, such as model predictive control and reinforcement learning, across a diverse set of energy management problems. The understandability of TreeC's energy management strategies greatly improves the transparency and trustworthiness of machine learning-based energy management. 2) The real-world experiment highlights the need to validate energy management methods on actual hardware and not solely on simulations. Both TreeC and model predictive control performed worse than in simulations, due to, respectively, insufficiently representative training data and the real-time input data not being reliable enough. 3) Energy management algorithms reduce costs in electrified Belgian dwellings, particularly in poorly insulated dwellings, electric vehicle chargers, and photovoltaic installations. However, on average, these cost savings do not yet outweigh the current costs and effort required to implement such algorithms.

Bayesian optimization on amortized search space

Tristan Fauvel, Federico Pavone, Titouan Guillou, Pau Redon Muñoz, Chloé Geoffroy

In real-world black-box optimization problems, a mechanistic simulator is often available that approximately predicts the behavior of the system as a function of the parameters being optimized. Here, we show how an amortized inverse map of such a simulator can be used in Bayesian optimization to restrict the search space while using a Gaussian process kernel that is aware of the original problem geometry. This allows for efficiently leveraging the low intrinsic dimensionality of the optimization problem. We characterize its approximation bias and information gain, and derive an intrinsic-dimension-dependent regret rate under additional geometric and Gaussian Process assumptions. We illustrate the method with an application to drug dosing personalization in Parkinson's disease. We use, as a simulator, a pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic (PK/PD) model of a drug, a mechanistic model calibrated on population data with quantified parameter uncertainty. A dosing neural network is trained offline against the model, amortizing the map from patient PK/PD parameters to the regimen predicted to be optimal, and online Bayesian optimization then searches over this learned, low-dimensional representation rather than over raw dosages. Numerical simulations demonstrate the improved performance of our method in this application, as well as under simulator misspecification.

Perfumer: A Large Language Model-based Agentic Framework Accelerating Structural Relaxation and Electronic Band Prediction for First-Principle Calculation

Zhiwei Li, Yuhao Zhou, Yikai Yang, Haonan Wang, Qing Ye, Jiancheng Lv

First-principles structural relaxation and band calculations require substantial computation and expertise. Here we present Perfumer, a large language model-based agentic framework that couples Quantum ESPRESSO with two predictive models. Across tested structures, pre-relaxation with Perfumer-relax model followed by QE refinement yielded a 2.75-fold overall speedup over QE alone. Perfumer-band model combines a periodic graph neural network with a latent Hermitian spectral head returns 12 band energies at any requested wavevector, preserving reciprocal lattice periodicity and time-reversal symmetry without constructing Hamiltonian. Trained on 125,153 self-curated band structures, Perfumer-band model reaches a median per-material mean absolute error of 0.157 eV on 2,607 test materials, including lanthanide, actinide and magnetic compounds, from held-out composition families spanning 85 elements, taking about 97 ms per material on two consumer CPU cores. The agent turns natural-language requests into relaxation-to-band workflows, from a single uploaded structure to automatically constructed new candidates and returns predicted properties.

GraphEP: A Calibrated Data System and Benchmark for Learning Cardiac Electrophysiology

Matteo Baldan, Chiara D'Ercoli, James Rowbottom, Pietro Liò, Stefania Fresca

High-fidelity simulations generate spatiotemporal data that are difficult to obtain experimentally, yet their outputs are rarely ready for scientific machine learning. Cardiac electrophysiology (EP) exemplifies this challenge: data reside on anatomy-dependent volumetric meshes, propagation follows anisotropic material fields, and relevant events span multiple temporal scales. We introduce GraphEP, a calibrated data system that converts cardiac meshes and simulation protocols into compact, graph-native trajectories of full-field transmembrane potential. GraphEP includes 732 left-ventricular simulations across 20 anatomies, with healthy and scarred substrates of varying severity and multiple pacing configurations. Each sample provides geometry, tissue and material features, stimulation, connectivity, derived activation targets, and provenance in PyTorch Geometric format. We benchmark four inductive biases for predicting transmembrane potential Vm(t): local message passing, global neural operators, multi-scale hierarchical graph transformers (HGT), and physiology-informed models. The latter include HGT-Lag, which separates activation timing from action-potential morphology, and the stateful Graph Aliev-Panfilov Action-Potential ODE (GAP2ODE), which evolves excitation and recovery autoregressively with frame-wise stimulation and graph diffusion. Results show that wider spatial communication improves on local message passing, while physiological structure provides the strongest inductive bias: HGT-Lag and GAP2ODE lead on complementary metrics by explicitly modeling propagation, recovery, and temporal state. GraphEP offers a reproducible pathway from mechanistic cardiac EP simulations to analysis-ready data and a controlled benchmark for learning on irregular anatomical geometries.

FEA solver integration of a composite World Model with a Spiking Neural Network memory Block for computationally efficient modelling of defects and variabilities

Bassam El Said, Adam Sobey

Each fibre-reinforced composite structure has a unique material architecture. The manufacturing methods used and the presence of defects lead to local variations which require material-scale models that can resolve the response in real time at every location in the structure. Concurrent multi-scale schemes provide this capability, but at a prohibitive computational cost for large structures as the number of integration points and time increments scales with size. Surrogates have been studied extensively as replacements for material-scale models to mitigate these computational limitations. A surrogate must represent an arbitrary local architecture including defects, track the irreversible material state over an unknown number of increments, remain stable when driven recursively by its own predictions, and fit within the memory available at every integration point. Here, a Composites World Model based on a Multi-Modal Spiking Transformer architecture is designed to meet these requirements. The proposed architecture combines a material encoder, a Spiking Neural Network memory block that tracks the long-term internal state of the material, and transformer blocks that learn the short-term applied strain state. The system is trained in three stages: material architecture encoding, base training on short time windows, and recursive fine-tuning with scheduled sampling to suppress cumulative error drift. The trained model is disassembled at inference and coupled to a non-linear FE solver, enabling structural scale simulation of composites containing embedded defects. Benchmarks against experimental results for IM7/8552 laminates under tension, compression and transverse shear, for pristine and wrinkled composites, show that the SNN memory block successfully tracks the material internal state and results in prediction errors between 0.4% and 4.8%.

Cost-Aware Agentic Feature Optimization System for Fleet-Scale Ranking Models

Chang Yang, Yao Xuan, Wendan Yan, Nhat Le, Jiang Liu, Longhao Jin

Large Web platforms operate large fleets of ranking models, each selecting features from a shared pool of tens of thousands. We present an end-to-end agentic system that optimizes features across the fleet under resource constraints shared across models and those set per model, such as feature pipeline cost and serving throughput (QPS), respectively. In this system, LLM agents propose hypotheses, and deterministic tools execute experiments and verify them. For each model, an LLM generates and shortlists cross features from a combinatorial candidate space, and all features are then ranked by several importance methods. Feature blending mixes these rankings into a single selection, using Pareto fronts across tasks for multi-task models. An LLM Researcher agent then tunes the feature selection in 10--25 asynchronous trials under resource caps, and a trimming step keeps the pipeline cost within budget. Across production models, the system improves normalized entropy by 0.31% on average within each model's budget, and the trimming step cuts pipeline cost by 2.3% with no regression beyond measurement noise. The optimized models were launched, and in online A/B tests they improved a primary online metric by 0.20%.

Training LLMs to Follow Reasoning Instructions with Minimal Edits

Alexandra Yakovleva, Luat Gia Khoi Nguyen, Juho Kannala, Harri Valpola, Alexander Ilin

Large language models increasingly rely on extended reasoning traces, yet the training signals used to shape this behavior remain coarse. Supervised fine-tuning on full trajectories forces the model to imitate an entire demonstration, overconstraining its output distribution, while trajectory-level reinforcement learning provides only weak credit assignment, rewarding or penalizing whole completions without identifying where they succeed or fail. We propose edit-based training, a general framework for learning from a model's own trajectories via localized textual corrections. Given a sampled completion, an editor identifies the first point of failure under a specified criterion and proposes a minimal replacement span. The model is then supervised only on these edited spans. By design, this concentrates supervision where the model's reasoning first breaks down and aims to preserve its existing reasoning patterns elsewhere. The method applies both offline and online, with the latter continually adapting supervision to the model's evolving failure modes. We instantiate this approach on reasoning instruction following with Qwen3-32B on the ReasonIF benchmark. Compared to full-trajectory imitation and trajectory-level reinforcement learning baselines, edit-based training substantially improves compliance with the reasoning instruction while largely preserving the model's skills on untargeted tasks. These results suggest that localized correction is an effective complement to these approaches for shaping how LLMs reason.

ClosureBench: A Benchmark for Reasoning under Closure Assumptions

Francesco Belardinelli, Flavio Corradini, Matteo Leonesi

AI agents built on large language models (LLMs) often answer questions from incomplete records. Missing information might be interpreted as unknown or false, depending on the completeness of the source. Current evaluations fail to distinguish these two aspects, as they are usually interwoven with other reasoning demands. We present ClosureBench, a benchmark that tests whether LLMs follow the stated rules for dealing with missing information. Every base scenario is tested under OWA, CWA, and LCWA, and only the treatment of missing information changes between variants. ClosureBench provides a verified base benchmark as well as agent extensions for action choice, source scope, and updates. A model is considered correct on a base scenario only if it provides correct answers in all of its closure variants. Across 960 instances, overall accuracy can mask substantial closure mistakes. Models achieve overall accuracy from 71.18% to 99.86% across replicated runs, but their core switch accuracy, measured on the scenarios whose gold label actually changes with the contract, varies between 18.23% and 99.31%. Large discrepancies emerge under LCWA. For example, some agents choose unknown despite the prompt indicating that missing information should be treated as false. Our findings demonstrate that evaluations of AI agents should not only measure whether agents can reason over existing facts but also whether they use the appropriate assumption when information is absent.

Our work demonstrates the effectiveness of UGC GEO placement at influencing generative engine outcomes, and reveals content-level defenses as insufficient against coordinated UGC placements, where account-level and platform-level signals are necessary for proper defense.

Active Feature Acquisition by Distilling an Oracle Teacher

Yang Meng, Zixin Ding, Robert L. Grossman, Yuxin Chen

Acquiring every feature of an instance is often costly: a measurement can carry a monetary price, a time delay, or a privacy cost. Active Feature Acquisition (AFA) addresses this by learning a policy that decides, per instance, which features to acquire under a budget. Existing AFA methods train this policy by optimizing a self-constructed utility target rather than distilling an explicit teacher. We construct this teacher explicitly. At training time the label and the unobserved feature values are available, and together they determine the teacher in closed form: an oracle that ranks every candidate by its effect on the downstream predictor's error. OracleImitator distills this ranking into a deployable policy by soft-target imitation. Across multimodal medical diagnosis and the AFABench benchmark, OracleImitator matches or outperforms existing AFA methods across the budget range, recovering 90% of the attainable accuracy gain at under a quarter of the acquisition cost. Because the teacher is an explicit algorithmic object rather than an implicit optimization target, we can also measure directly how much of its ranking is recoverable by a policy restricted to what is visible at deployment.

Successor features separate dynamics from task-specific reward, but their effectiveness depends on the choice of base features. Taking the identification of entorhinal grid cells with eigenvectors of the successor representation as a design principle, we build this basis from eigenvectors of the transition matrix of a fixed reference policy. With such a basis, the successor features at a state-action pair are a linear combination of the base features at that same pair, which is not true in general, and the coefficients are known in closed form: each successor feature is its base feature multiplied by $θ_i = 1/(1-γλ_i)$, where $λ_i$ is its eigenvalue. The long-term return is thus predicted from the same features as the immediate reward, no successor-feature network or replay buffer is needed, and adapting to a new task reduces to a linear regression on the reward. The agent acts greedily on the action values of the reference policy, which is one step of policy improvement over it and suffices to reach a single goal. In a two-goal gridworld, with the uniform random walk as reference policy, whose eigenvectors are the low-frequency Laplacian eigenvectors, agents built this way maintain near-maximal return across every change of goal, whereas SimpleSF and DQN lose most of their return at each change.

We present a new adaptive stepsize rule that biases gradient descent toward flatter minima in both theory and practice, and establish the first uniform-in-time guarantees for both sharpness and loss reduction for a broad class of locally quadratic objectives. Our critically damped Polyak (CDP) stepsizes multiply the classical Polyak rule by $4$ and a mild damping factor. The factor $4$ is critical in that it produces equioscillations around the manifold of minimizers $\mathcal{M}$ to leading order. Damping provably drives convergence to $\mathcal{M}$. CDP thus operates at the edge of stability by adapting its stepsizes to local curvature. Once the loss is small, its projected iterates follow gradient descent on $\log{λ_1(\nabla^2 \mathcal{L})}$ along $\mathcal{M}$ to leading order, paralleling the flatness bias of normalized gradient descent (Arora et al., 2022). Unlike normalized gradient descent, CDP step lengths shrink with the distance to $\mathcal{M}$, enabling convergence to an interpolating solution that locally minimizes sharpness at finite learning rate. We establish our theory for a broad class of non-negative losses with locally quadratic Morse-Bott structure near $\mathcal{M}$, capturing regular interpolation regimes in smooth overparameterized neural networks. Empirically, CDP stepsizes produce a flattening effect similar to that of Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM, Foret et al., 2021), reducing sharpness even in settings beyond our theory, including nonlinear teacher-student networks and fine-tuning of pre-trained LLMs.
