Fixed-point acceleration on Neural Wasserstein manifolds

Vitalii Aksenov, Sebastian Gutierrez Hernandez

We propose a fixed-point acceleration method for optimization over probability measures, applicable to a variety of machine learning problems. We represent probability measures as pushforwards of a reference distribution through a neural network map and equip the parameter space with the induced pullback Wasserstein metric. This geometric structure allows natural gradient descent to be interpreted as a fixed-point iteration on a Riemannian manifold. We accelerate this iteration using Riemannian Anderson mixing method, which exploits historical residuals to capture approximate second-order information without forming Hessians. In the present parametric setting, the manifold is represented by Euclidean coordinates endowed with a point-dependent metric, simplifying retraction and vector transport. We evaluate the method on multimodal sampling, PDE-based inverse problems, aggregation dynamics, Bayesian logistic regression, and generative modeling. Across the tested tasks, Anderson mixing reduces the number of natural-gradient iterations and achieves competitive performance relative to SGD and Adam in terms of both loss-gradient evaluations and wall-clock time. These results suggest that fixed-point acceleration is an efficient tool for improving the computational efficiency of training on parametric Wasserstein methods.

Inverse design of superconductors and semiconductors with differentiable foundation models

Cristóbal Méndez, Justin Tahmassebpur, Tomás A. Arias

Designing a crystal for a target property requires choosing its elements, stoichiometry, and atomic structure together, yet gradients exist only for the continuous structural variables. We introduce differentiable Equiformer fast effective-atom theory (DEFEAT), which lets a pretrained EquiformerV3 foundation model accept continuous species weights and site occupancies without retraining on fractional data, thereby enabling gradient-based materials optimization. Gradients from fine-tuned property models and a machine-learned energy penalty then guide updates to composition, atom count, atomic positions, and lattice vectors in a single optimization. In a search for phonon-mediated superconductors, density-functional perturbation theory on 45 structurally novel designs gives isotropic Allen-Dynes critical temperatures above 10 K for 39; the highest is 24.0 K for ZrTiNb2CN3, and H4LiPd3 (23.2 K) lies on the static DFT hull. In searches for photovoltaic absorbers, SCAN calculations confirm ten novel crystal structures with band gaps within 20% of a 1.34 eV target on their respective raw or empirically corrected scales, all within 70 meV/atom of the DFT hull, and show that the systematic gap error of the training functional propagates into the designs unless the objective corrects for it. Quasiharmonic free energies from machine-learned potentials further show that finite-temperature competition with solid phases can reorder candidates relative to the static hull.

Discovering formally verified quantum programs with AI

Sergey Shirobokov, Ben Jaderberg, Katerina Hristova, George Tsoukalas, Johannes Bausch, Eric Wieser

The timeline by which quantum computers can solve breakthrough problems in science depends on improved quantum algorithms as much as on better hardware. Such algorithmic advances are typically achieved through many years of expert human collaboration. In mathematics and computer science, large language models have accelerated progress through automated discovery loops. However, quantum algorithms can only be evaluated by classical computers on restricted or small instances. Here we demonstrate automated discovery of quantum programs beyond these regimes including order-of-magnitude improvements on scientific applications. To achieve this, we build a quantum program library in the Lean theorem prover in which large language model agents synthesize novel programs against formal problem specifications and verify their correctness and cost. Agent teams collaborate, sharing results and knowledge through wikis and discussing ideas at organized conferences. Our results improve on prior baselines for parallel search (up to 1.4x), quantum arithmetic (290x), and simulations relevant to fusion, photochemistry and drug-metabolism by 11x, 8x and 2.5x respectively. Combining human and artificial intelligence at scale, under the rigor of mathematical proof, lets researchers design algorithms that today's classical and quantum computers cannot test, shortening the path to practical quantum computation.

MemoryDojo: Benchmarking Poisoning Through LLM Agent Memory

David H. Yang, Blake Bullwinkel, Amanda Minnich, Mohammad Mohammadi Amiri, Giorgio Severi

LLM agents benefit from using memory systems to improve performance on long-horizon, multi-session tasks. However, memory introduces a critical attack surface. Malicious instructions encountered in one session may be stored and propagated through memory before being retrieved and acted upon in later sessions. Because memory systems differ in how they process and retrieve information, the downstream effects of poisoning can vary significantly across systems. To quantify the effects of poisoning across different memory architectures, we introduce MemoryDojo, a benchmark for tracing the lifecycle of poisoned information through agent memory and measuring its influence on subsequent agent behavior. MemoryDojo is easily extensible, consists of 155 multi-session long-horizon trajectories covering four domains, and supports five different memory systems. Through our evaluation, we find that all memory systems are susceptible to memory poisoning, with substantial variation in downstream effects. We further propose a memory-aware poison optimization process that increases the poisoning efficacy, highlighting differences between memory architectures. We hope that MemoryDojo will support research on understanding and mitigating agentic memory risks.

On the Reliability of Majority-Vote Pseudo-Labels in Test-Time Reinforcement Learning

Haoyu Wang, Ru Wang, Yuanyuan Wang, Jiaxian Guo, Mingming Gong

Test-time reinforcement learning (TTRL) adapts pretrained language models to unlabeled target-domain data using self-generated pseudo-labels, whose reliability remains poorly understood. We analyze majority-vote supervision for TTRL with online Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and decompose its error into population-majority bias and prompt-dependent finite-rollout variation. Because population-majority bias is generally unidentifiable without external supervision, we focus on mitigating the downstream effects of finite-rollout variation. We propose \emph{Prompt-Conditional Distributionally Robust DPO (PC-Dr.DPO)}, which conditions within-prompt robust reweighting on observed vote reliability to make updates from sampling-sensitive prompts more conservative. We further introduce a cache-based mechanism to reduce self-supervised forgetting during online adaptation. Across six mathematical and knowledge-intensive reasoning benchmarks and five model backbones, PC-Dr.DPO matches or outperforms the strongest TTRL baseline in 24 of 30 settings.

A Tight Hierarchy for Chain of Thought

Thanh Le, A. Pavan, N. V. Vinodchandran

Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning has emerged as a powerful paradigm for extending the computational capabilities of transformer-based models. A growing body of recent work has begun to uncover the fine-grained computational power of transformers augmented with CoT, as well as their connections to classical complexity theory. In this work, we advance this line of research by establishing the first hierarchy theorem for CoT-based computation. Our main result shows that for any reasonable resource bound $r(n) \geq n$ and any $\varepsilon > 0$, \[ \mathrm{CoT}(r(n)) \subsetneq \mathrm{CoT}(r(n)^{1+\varepsilon}), \] where $\mathrm{CoT}(r(n))$ denotes the class of decision problems solvable by transformers using $O(r(n))$ CoT reasoning steps on inputs of length $n$. Our result shows that increasing the number of CoT reasoning steps provably yields strictly greater computational power, establishing CoT steps as a fundamental resource in transformer-based computation. Unlike classical hierarchy theorems, which are typically proved via diagonalization, our approach leverages known coarse-grained simulations between CoT-augmented transformers and Turing machines in both directions. Building on these connections, we adapt the classical padding technique from complexity theory to this setting to obtain a tight hierarchy. A key technical contribution of our work is the implementation of padding within the transformer-based CoT model.

TensorMutation: Learning Weight-Space Mutations of Deep Learning Models on Neural Graphs

Zaheed Ahmed, Philip Makedonski, Jens Grabowski

Mutation testing for deep learning (DL) faces a cost-realism trade-off. The pre-training approach produces realistic but computationally expensive mutants, whereas existing post-training operators modify trained models efficiently but produce mutants that less closely resemble real faults. This study asks whether the trade-off is necessary: can the weight-space effect of a fault-informed pre-training mutation operator be predicted from a trained model, without retraining it? We answer this question with TensorMutation, which represents a trained model as a neural graph and uses a mutation-conditioned graph neural network (GNN) to predict parameter changes. The GNN is trained offline and reused across unseen models so that TensorMutation can generate executable mutants without retraining. We construct a corpus of 25,252 original-mutant pairs from 71 multilayer perceptron (MLP) classifiers using 11 fault-informed pre-training operators, including 25 newly curated subjects. TensorMutation partially reproduces the behavioral effects of pre-training mutations on nine held-out subjects, and direct weight-space supervision significantly improves weight-space fidelity. On 12 real-fault subjects, TensorMutation achieves the highest median behavioral similarity among the evaluated approaches. The improvement over the post-training tool DeepMutation++ is statistically significant (Holm-adjusted p=0.018), whereas the differences from the pre-training tool DeepCrime and the norm-matched random control are not significant. These results show that fault-informed pre-training mutations leave learnable structure in weight space that transfers to unseen models.

BiLoGo: Reducing the smoothing of localised responses in Transformer-based neural operators for sheet forming

Yingxue Zhao, Haoran Li, Haosu Zhou, Tobias Pfaff, Nan Li

Trial-and-error FE simulation of sheet forming could be computationally expensive, neural-network-based learned simulators are used to accelerate design. These simulators should accurately capture localised responses, such as thinning around sharp radii or near ribs, which determine formability. Transformer-based neural operators propagate information efficiently across the mesh but tend to smooth such localised responses. This study develops BiLoGo, a Bipartite Local-Global learned simulator with an FE-guided design that connects local node-element message passing with a Transformer-based neural operator for long-range information exchange. BiLoGo has three modules: a bidirectional node-element GraphSAGE model that learns nodal-elemental coupling on a bipartite graph and predicts each quantity on its native FE entity without interpolation; a dual-stream Transformer-based neural operator that processes blank deformation and tool contact with separate token sets, preventing localised contact information from being averaged with the whole-blank state; and a fusion and element update module that combines global and local features via gated residual fusion and updates element features conditioned on element-node kinematics. Evaluated on a dome-shaped cold forming case and two corner-shaped hot stamping cases (one with ribs), BiLoGo achieves the lowest positional and threshold-based thinning errors on all three cases compared with single-level and hierarchical graph-based models and Transformer-based neural operators. Near the ribs, the smoothing index of predicted thinning increases from 0.41-0.56 for Transformer-based neural operators to 0.94 for BiLoGo-U. Compared with the hierarchical graph network CAtt-BiUGNN, BiLoGo-T reduces mean positional error by 15.5% on the Corner case and BiLoGo-U by 8.3% on the Corner+Rib case, with 66-69% less training memory.

Decision-focused learning trains prediction models to improve downstream decisions, but many methods require costly solver calls or optimal-solution labels. We propose a solver-free decision-focused learning framework for NP-hard edge-selection problems with uncertain parameters in the objective, the constraints, or both. A neural optimization layer is first trained without solution labels, using problem objectives and constraint penalties. Graph-informed edge scoring and iterative refinement encourage concentrated and structurally consistent continuous decisions. The layer is then frozen, and a prediction model is trained through its continuous output using a problem-specific loss evaluated under observed parameters. At deployment, the predictor, optimization layer, and a lightweight decoder produce decisions without solving the original NP-hard problem. Experiments on the asymmetric traveling salesperson and endpoint-free orienteering problems show consistently better decision quality than prediction-focused learning and improvements over most evaluated decision-focused baselines. Our framework requires less training time than the evaluated decision-focused baselines, even when optimization-layer pretraining is included. Neural deployment also produces decisions faster than solver-based deployment, offering a better quality-efficiency trade-off. The layer also reduces training cost in existing decision-focused methods when used as an approximate optimization oracle.

FiLSim remains accurate on graphs with more than 150{,}000 nodes, where the baseline methods run out of memory or fail to learn, and scores all test pairs within seconds on a single GPU.

LambdaMix jointly trains one low-rank adapter per objective over a frozen base model. One preference vector selects the adapter mixture that generates responses and weights the separately normalized rewards used to update the adapters. With architecture and initialization fixed, varying adapter mixtures and objective weights together gives wider score ranges and higher coverage than varying either alone.

To study how best to use monitors to mitigate reward hacking, we introduce and open source three realistic environments where Qwen3-4B reward hacks: a coding environment hackable via test overwriting, a medical chat environment hackable via sycophancy, and a biography generation environment hackable via hallucination. We apply our findings to build better reward hacking monitors for the medical chat and biography generation environments that improve upon naive baselines to reduce reward hacking rates across seeds to before-training levels in some interventions. Our results demonstrate that our takeaways translate to new settings and that better monitor intervention designs are possible.

Attend at the Right Resolution: Head-Adaptive Sparse Attention via Query-Key Interaction Structure

Wajih Hassan Raza, Ahsan Bilal, Yidan Shen, Renjie Hu, Xin Fu, Yu Wen

Attention is a major scalability bottleneck in large language models (LLMs), with its computational cost and key-value (KV) memory traffic becoming increasingly prohibitive as context length grows. Sparse attention addresses this bottleneck by restricting each query to a subset of the context, but its effectiveness hinges on identifying relevant regions both cheaply and reliably. Existing training-free methods primarily rely on runtime relevance estimates, with selection dimensionality and granularity typically governed by fixed heuristics rather than the intrinsic interaction structure of pretrained attention heads. We take a different perspective: the query-key operator of each pretrained head defines a characteristic response structure that determines the resolution needed for sparse selection, both across feature directions and across context tokens. Building on this insight, we propose Head-Adaptive Sparse Attention (HASA), a training-free sparse attention framework that jointly adapts selection resolution along these two dimensions. Along the feature dimension, the singular spectrum of each head's query-key operator determines an effective rank, yielding a compact, head-specific response space. Along the sequence dimension, neighboring regions are progressively merged according to the directional coherence of their token responses in this same space, while heterogeneous regions remain separate. Exact attention is then computed using the original full-dimensional KV states of the selected regions. HASA captures 88.1% of dense-attention mass and, compared with UNIQUE, a strong recent sparse-attention baseline, improves accuracy by up to 3.47 and 2.57 points on RULER and LongBench-Pro, respectively, and accelerates self-attention by up to 1.32x, while achieving up to 14.07x speedup over dense attention.

A Generalized Bayesian Approach to Clustering with Dimension Reduction

Shen Jia, Weipeng Huang, Geng Yang, Eoin Gallagher, Tin Lok James Ng

Generalized Bayes product partition models provide a probabilistic counterpart to loss-based clustering. They update a prior over partitions through an exponentiated clustering loss rather than a likelihood, so allocation uncertainty can be quantified without a full generative model for the data. However, their performance depends on the space in which the loss is evaluated. For high-dimensional observations, a squared Euclidean loss on the raw features can be dominated by irrelevant coordinates or fail to reflect cluster structure lying near a nonlinear low-dimensional manifold. We propose an extension in which an autoencoder and the partition are learned together, so that the space in which the clustering loss is evaluated is shaped by the clustering itself. Our generalized posterior combines a reconstruction loss, which keeps the low-dimensional representation faithful to the observed data, with a within-cluster sum of squares in the latent representation, which encourages cluster separation. Posterior computation alternates between Gibbs sampling over partitions and gradient-based updates of the autoencoder network, with the learning rate estimated jointly. We illustrate the approach through simulation studies and applications to four benchmark datasets.

Autonomous Particle Image Velocimetry Using Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Reinforcement Learning

Chang Rajani, Luke Sebastien Marsden, Stephan Kunz, Claudio Mucignat, Ivan Lunati

Particle image velocimetry (PIV) reconstructs a flow field from the motion of tracer particles but the reconstruction quality is bounded by how well the flow structure of interest is resolved in the images. The acquisition position is typically fixed by the operator before the measurement. We cast camera placement as a sequential decision-making problem and train a reinforcement learning agent to control a 6-DoF manipulator equipped with a camera, enabling it to locate, frame and resolve a hydrofoil wake in a water tunnel. The agent commands the camera pose and observes the scene only through the velocity field and uncertainty estimated from time-separated image pairs. It is trained entirely in a photorealistic simulation of our water tunnel, with wakes generated by Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) simulations of hydrofoils with varying profiles, positions and angles of attack. After zero-shot transfer to the real water tunnel, the policy reliably locates and frames the wakes of randomly placed hydrofoils. It resolves the wake at a higher resolution than a fixed camera view and with lower error in the measured flow statistics, in roughly half the time of a full scan and with far fewer image pairs.

Diffusion-free Score Matching for Exact Conditional Sampling

Thomas Wedenig, Robert Peharz

The success of modern score-based generative models is largely driven by diffusion-based training, which learns a collection of time-indexed score fields corresponding to progressively noisier versions of the data distribution. While such models achieve impressive sample quality, leveraging them for sound probabilistic inference---particularly sampling from arbitrary conditionals of the model---remains challenging. This difficulty arises because conditioning information is only available for clean data and not for the higher noise levels required to generate exact conditional samples. In this paper, we introduce DIffusion-free SCOre matching (DISCO), a principled approach that sidesteps the need for time-dependent score fields altogether. Our method is based on an objective that, while reminiscent of diffusion-based training, estimates only a single score of the (slightly perturbed) data distribution, for which the exact conditional score is readily obtained. In experiments, DISCO is competitive with state-of-the-art diffusion models in terms of sample quality. More importantly, DISCO yields a more faithful representation of the underlying data distribution and---crucially---enables accurate sampling from arbitrary conditionals, outperforming standard heuristic samplers. This capability opens the door to principled and flexible probabilistic reasoning with score-based models.

FlowMoP: Stochastic Multi-Person Motion Prediction

Aadya Agrawal, Ho Kei Cheng, Alexander Schwing

Predicting the future motion of multiple people is inherently stochastic: the same observed history admits many plausible continuations depending on intent, coordination, and social context. Yet the dominant paradigm in multi-person 3D motion prediction remains deterministic, with existing methods producing a fixed number of outputs per agent. In contrast, we present a conditional flow matching framework that jointly generates diverse future motions for multiple agents, modeling both global trajectories and root-relative poses. A B-spline reparameterization compresses the generative state space while enforcing temporal smoothness, and a dual-stream transformer conditions each agent's predictions on observed neighbor motion for interaction-aware forecasting. Our method achieves state-of-the-art performance on CMU-Mocap (UMPM) and 3DPW, outperforming deterministic baselines on joint position error, pose error, and final displacement error despite producing a full predictive distribution. We additionally establish the first motion prediction baseline on WorldPose, a large-scale professional soccer dataset, where social conditioning demonstrably concentrates predictive uncertainty along interaction-constrained directions.

One Run Is Not Enough: A Monte Carlo Protocol for Evaluating LLM Reliability on Data Science Tasks

Wenqi Ni, Ozan Evkaya, Miguel de Carvalho

Large language models (LLMs) have shown promise for automating data science workflows. Benchmarks such as DataSciBench (2026) and StatQA (2024) have advanced the evaluation of their capabilities across data science and statistical tasks. Assessing their reliability requires examining both the quality of their responses and their consistency across repeated runs, task types, and difficulty levels. With this aim, we introduce DS-QA, a benchmark comprising 100 data science and statistics questions drawn from UK university courses, spanning 16 datasets and three difficulty levels. We develop a three-layer evaluation framework that combines general response properties (runtime, verbosity and text similarity), coarse-grained metrics (accuracy, code executability and completeness), and task-specific metrics. We evaluate OpenAI models using a Monte Carlo protocol with 100 repeated runs. The results reveal substantial variability in responses to data visualisation and supervised learning tasks, particularly at medium and hard difficulty levels, highlighting the importance of evaluating consistency alongside response quality. We also find that an increase of 0.1 in the word verbosity ratio corresponds to an increase of about 0.013 in the token verbosity ratio.

Encoding Memory in World Models with Test-Time Model-Based Reinforcement Learning

Maxime Burchi, Fayaz Ali Dharejo, Radu Timofte

Model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) approaches have demonstrated strong performance across diverse domains in recent years, learning accurate world models from agent trajectories. However, despite this progress, current approaches still struggle to surpass human performance on tasks that require long-term memory and planning. In this work, we address the problem of long-term memory in MBRL by moving beyond the commonly studied paradigm of architecture design. We introduce Test-Time Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (TT-MBRL), using gradient descent at test time to encode the memory of episode observations into world model parameters. This enables the agent to learn a distinct world model for each episode, improving memory capabilities compared to traditional approaches that solely rely on contextual memory. We first establish a proof of concept on a simplified two-dimensional variant of the Memory Maze environment, which we refer to as Memory Maze 2D, showing consistent improvements across different world model architectures. We then demonstrate the effectiveness of TT-MBRL in the 3D domain, achieving new state-of-the-art results on the Memory Maze benchmark. Finally, we show that TT-MBRL maintains strong performance on non-memory benchmarks such as DeepMind Control and Atari 100k.
