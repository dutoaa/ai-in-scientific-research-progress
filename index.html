A Multi-Agent LLM System for Automated Tuning of Specialized Neural Network Models

Ivan Moloshnikov, Dmitry Kunitsyn, Alexander Sboev, Maksim Skorokhodov, Roman Rybka, Yuanchao Liu

Many machine learning approaches are two-level: an expert selects a class of methods, within this class the choice of key components is critical and usually manual. This paper addresses the automatic tuning of these components and proposes a framework transferable across models of different nature. The multi-agent large language model (LLM) framework has three roles---analyst, coder, and recorder~---with an experiment log and a sandboxed execution environment. Unlike systems that optimize the entire ML script or search architectures in a discrete space, the framework operates over an open but semantically narrow space: the agent generates code only for the specified components, leaving the overall network structure unchanged. The approach is validated in two directions: tuning a classification spiking neural network with correlation learning, and solving high-order differential equations with Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINN). Across five tasks---spiking classification on MNIST and Digits, and boundary-value problems for the generalized nonlinear Schrödinger equation (GNLSE) and Kuramoto--Sivashinsky (KS) equations---the agent outperforms the baselines: classification accuracy is 13 percentage points (MNIST) and 6 percentage points (Digits) higher; the error against the analytical single-soliton solution is reduced approximately 2491-fold; the invariant-preservation error drops approximately 325-fold in the selected GNLSE run with a Gaussian perturbation and by more than 100-fold on average over repeated initializations; and the mass drift drops 14-fold for the KS problem with a Gaussian perturbation. The framework thus tunes key components on tasks of different nature without changing its internal structure.

Matched-Context Group Policy Optimization for LLM Agent Training

Chenxu Zhang, Yaorui Shi, Wenlong Zhang, Jianchao Tan, Zihan Wang, Yiming Cheng

Training long-horizon language model agents requires assigning credit to intermediate decisions from sparse terminal feedback. Within a finite group of sibling rollouts, useful action comparisons depend on their context, evidence for both alternatives, and how that evidence enters learning. We propose Matched-Context Group Policy Optimization (MCGPO), a procedure for constructing weighted action preferences from already collected trajectories. MCGPO defines comparison contexts with pre-action state summaries and observed rubric progress, annotated by task rules. Two-action support selects among ordered exact and coarse matching rules. Fixed folds separate candidate decisions from the terminal outcomes used to orient their preferences, and support-dependent evidence weights scale an auxiliary pairwise objective alongside shared GraphGPO task learning. MCGPO reuses rollout data and each response's original history, preserving critic-free training. Across six environment-backbone settings with three training seeds each, MCGPO improves mean success over matched GraphGPO: by 0.47-1.46 percentage points on ALFWorld and WebShop with Qwen2.5-1.5B and 7B, and by 3.96 and 5.16 points on WebShop with Qwen3-4B and 8B. The ALFWorld gains build on baseline success above 92%, where remaining headroom is limited. Integrated success improves in three of four Qwen2.5 settings. Three-seed factorial controls support opposite-fold evidence and evidence weighting; joint state-rubric matching achieves the highest mean in all four representation-control settings.

Goal-Oriented Bundle Management and Flow Control for Deep-Space Communications via Reinforcement Learning

Sajjad Baghaeea, Aimin Lib, Shanmugarajan Balasubramanian, Elif Uysal

Deep-space communications operate under intermittent connectivity, long propagation delays, and relay storage constraints. In such networks, the Bundle Protocol enables store-carry-forward communication, requiring relay nodes to manage limited storage and scarce contact opportunities. Relay-side scheduling and dropping decisions are inherently goal-oriented: transmission resources should be allocated to bundles most valuable to downstream mission objectives. Conventional schedulers, such as FIFO, do not explicitly optimize task utility and may provide unfair service across heterogeneous planetary sources. This paper presents a closed-loop modeling and learning framework for relay-side bundle and flow management in a contact-driven deep-space network, where lunar and Martian updates are forwarded through a shared-buffer relay to Earth. We quantify destination-side information freshness using excess age $Δ_i^e(t)=[Δ_i(t)-d_i(t)]^+$, where $Δ_i(t)$ denotes the Age of Information of source $i$ and $d_i(t)$ is a source-dependent delay baseline. Based on this metric, we define a goal-oriented loss that supports source-dependent nonlinear penalties, allowing prioritization according to mission relevance. We formulate relay control as a constrained semi-Markov decision process (CSMDP) and solve it using masked Lagrangian Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). Feasibility-aware action masking restricts transmissions to bundles that can complete within the remaining contact window. DTNSim simulations indicate improved age-based utility and inter-flow fairness compared with baseline schedulers. The proposed framework jointly optimizes relay-side bundle scheduling, dropping, and flow control under contact-driven dynamics with explicit fairness considerations across heterogeneous sources.

PhySeqNet: physics-informed fluorescent foci detection for BARseq spatial transcriptomics

Neha Goswami, John Hover, Renae Galluccio, Yichen Wu, Huiqing Zhan, Xiaoyin Chen

BARseq enables spatially resolved gene-expression profiling across intact tissue, but its sensitivity depends on accurate detection and decoding of dense fluorescent rolonies. Existing spot-calling approaches can be conservative where signals overlap, while supervised deep learning is limited by the lack of reliable manual ground truth in real tissue images. Here we present PhySeqNet, a physics-informed deep learning framework for annotation-free fluorescent foci detection in BARseq. PhySeqNet trains U-Net detectors entirely on semi-synthetic images generated from an instrument-matched optical model combined with real sample-free experimental noise, and is integrated into pyBARseq, a modular Python pipeline for BARseq analysis. PhySeqNet achieved high detection performance on held-out simulated data across wavelength bands. On real tissue images, where ground truth is unavailable, we evaluated detections by forward-model reconstruction: PhySeqNet with conservative barcode-aware non-maxima suppression (NMSB) achieved higher reconstruction fidelity (NCC and MS-SSIM) than the BarDensr single-shot estimator, tuned classical spot detectors, and a pretrained deep-learning detector. In the analyzed brain section, PhySeqNet with NMSB yielded a median of 51 counts per cell versus 47 for BarDensr, with a similar number of genes detected per cell. The recovered transcripts produced spatial expression patterns qualitatively consistent with Allen MERFISH reference data and preserved cell-type composition after scANVI label transfer. These results show that physics-informed simulation can train practical, annotation-free detectors that improve BARseq sensitivity without disrupting downstream biological interpretation.

Partially Informed Stochastic Optimization via Zeroth-Order Residual Tracking

El Mahdi Chayti, Alireza Mirrokni, Martin Jaggi

Many optimization problems provide direct access to only part of the gradient, while the remaining component must be inferred from function evaluations. This structure arises in performative prediction, performative reinforcement learning, and bilevel optimization with a black-box lower level. We introduce Partially Informed Stochastic Optimization (PISO), which combines the available gradient component with a running estimate of the residual. By centring each zeroth-order observation on this estimate, PISO makes the variance due to random probing depend on the tracking error rather than the residual's magnitude. This yields a randomized sketch iteration with an explicit contraction mechanism and a principled interpretation of momentum. For smooth nonconvex objectives, we establish convergence guarantees that depend on the residual's variation and initial magnitude, without requiring a uniform bound on the unavailable gradient component. With independently sampled function evaluations and Gaussian or spherical probes, PISO attains leading function-evaluation complexities of \(O(d^{2}\varepsilon^{-6})\) under Lipschitz gradients and \(O(d^{2}\varepsilon^{-5})\) under an additional Lipschitz-Hessian assumption. These rates require only one probe direction, two function samples, and one known-component sample per update. The analysis also quantifies how batching trades observations for fewer deployments. Across five settings, PISO variants improve on the tested baselines with comparable oracle access in three, reach a comparable solution faster in bilevel routing, and perform best among methods without response-model derivatives in performative reinforcement learning.

Dream to Remember: Continual Steering of Generalist Robot Policies with World Models

Bahey Tharwat, Iman Nematollahi, Marvin Alles, Xingyuan Zhang, Akshay L Chandra, Abhinav Valada

Generalist robot policies are trained with imitation learning and are bounded by the quality and coverage of their demonstrations. Reinforcement learning can improve and extend them, either by fine-tuning the policy weights or by training a small module to steer the input noise of a frozen generative policy. When tasks arrive sequentially, both approaches suffer from catastrophic forgetting. Fine-tuning on a task degrades prior tasks, and steering only shifts forgetting to the shared steering module. We propose Dream to Remember (D2R), which continually steers a frozen generalist policy toward new tasks, while retaining its earlier skills. As the steering module learns a new task, a frozen pretrained world model rehearses prior tasks through imagined rollouts, anchoring the module to its earlier behavior without a replay buffer or new data. When the world model is faithful enough, D2R learns the new task entirely in imagination. Otherwise, it learns the task in the environment while using the world model only to rehearse prior tasks. On CALVIN, D2R improves eight tasks in sequence entirely in imagination, raising average success from 63.7% to 74.8%, while LoRA fine-tuning on the same sequence drops it to 11.7%. With a frozen off-the-shelf generalist policy and world model, $π_0$ and A2World, D2R learns unseen LIBERO-90 tasks in the environment while keeping $π_0$'s performance on the LIBERO suites it was fine-tuned on, even though the world model is not accurate enough for policy optimization. Finally, on a real Franka robot, D2R improves three tasks in sequence entirely in imagination while retaining the earlier improvements.

Studying Comics at Scale with Artificial Intelligence: The PAGE Framework and Three Studies of American Comics and Japanese Manga

Emilio Ferrara

Comics are one of the great visual records of the twentieth century: hundreds of thousands of pages in which a mass readership saw its leaders, its wars and its heroes. Most of this record has never been studied as a whole; comics scholarship proceeds one book at a time. We argue that artificial intelligence, including generative models, can change this to the benefit of the medium itself. We propose PAGE, a framework that makes artificial intelligence an instrument for comics research. It parses every scan into panels, text and a bibliographic record; annotates each page with a vision-language model that follows a written codebook, as a human coder would; grounds samples of these readings in human annotation; and explains change across an archive, with estimates controlled for publisher and genre. We demonstrate the framework on 3,929 American comic books (1938--1956) and 87 volumes of Japanese manga, in three studies, each resting on a different capability. The first pairs text search with visual reading to recover how a mass medium portrayed political power. The second measures the page's visual form across an industry and recovers the hand of individual artists. The third follows who speaks across two comics traditions, checked against human annotation. Beyond research, the same instruments can make archives searchable by what is drawn, open comics to blind and low-vision readers, and help preserve fragile collections and credit uncredited artists. We outline a research agenda in which artificial intelligence strengthens the study, preservation and reach of the medium.

In this work, we study how the performance of the vision-inspired VARC and recursion-based TRM models is affected by pretraining and test-time training (TTT) budgets under a common inductive protocol on ARC-AGI-1. We cross five pretraining budgets with four TTT budgets for each test task. Each budget specifies a task-variant count and total sample presentations, defining the controlled training budgets. Increasing the TTT task-variant count consistently helps only TRM, whereas VARC often performs better with fewer variants. VARC also remains competitive without pretraining, while TRM benefits much more from it. These results show that pretraining and TTT budgets affect ARC evaluation and motivate reporting at comparable budgets.

Calibration in One Go: Jointly Learning Accurate and Calibrated Neural Networks

Nektarios Kalampalikis, Pablo M. Olmos, Isabel Valera

Neural networks (NNs), while highly flexible and versatile, are often poorly calibrated. Post-hoc calibration methods adjust the outputs of an already trained model using a separate calibration dataset and additional calibration parameters. This process adds overhead that requires separate optimization of the calibrator and subsequent model selection. In this paper, we show that standard (post-hoc) calibrators can be trained end-to-end with the backbone via a bilevel Stackelberg formulation. This formulation eliminates the need for a separate calibration set or a dedicated calibration loss by jointly optimizing accuracy and calibration using cross-entropy, introducing only one additional hyperparameter: the calibrator learning rate. Although our framework supports calibrators ranging from temperature and matrix scaling to NN layers, our results show that using the final classification layer itself as the calibrator yields accurate, well-calibrated models with no additional parameters. Our approach outperforms SOTA calibrators with minimal added complexity, while also avoiding the multiplicative overhead of separate calibration components in settings such as multi-task learning.

RSI-Anything: Unlocking Recursive Self-Improvement with World Models

Taoyong Cui, Pinxian Zeng, Wanghan Xu, Hao Xiao, Shuhan Xue, Qiang Gao

Recursive self-improvement (RSI) aims to transform interaction experience into persistent improvements, yet external feedback is costly and world-model predictions may become unreliable as policies evolve. We introduce RSI-Anything, a domain-general framework built on JEPA-Anything that uses predictive world models to guide action and learning-data selection. Observed feedback drives policy or workflow improvement and conditional world-model adaptation, while component and compatibility checks govern the retention of updated policy-model pairs. Across diverse computational and scientific domains, RSI-Anything demonstrates substantial gains. In code repair, with the coder fixed, success increases from 26.3% with a static verifier to 29.2% through predictive selection and 31.2% after world-model adaptation, at comparable test usage. Graphical user interface (GUI) evaluations showed higher task completion, while average actions decrease by 39.4%. Across five successive policy versions, laboratory-planning scores more than double from 21.80% to 46.01%. In reaction optimization, RSI-Anything achieves 96.15% mean best observed yield versus 82.16% for Bayesian optimization under the same 40-experiment budget. An end-to-end immune-science application further connects workflow evolution and predictive modeling with therapeutic hypothesis generation. These results demonstrate how predictive world models and retained system updates enable recursive improvement across diverse environments.

Constrained Probabilistic Numerics: Gaussian Process Solvers for Linear PDEs under Linear Inequality Constraints

Boyuan Deng, Kshitiz Upadhyay, Michael Shields

Gaussian process solvers for linear partial differential equations (PDEs) return posteriors rather than point estimates, but they condition only on equality information: the PDE residual, boundary data, and measurements. Many solutions of interest also obey inequality constraints, including nonnegativity, monotonicity, and absence of arbitrage, which equality conditioning cannot enforce. We prove that conditioning a Gaussian process prior jointly on linear equality observations and a finite-dimensional inequality event is equivalent to conditioning sequentially in terms of Borel measures on the solution space. The physics-informed posterior is therefore a valid prior for the inequality update at virtual points. The combined law is non-Gaussian yet computable through truncated-Gaussian moments, and neither the PDE conditioning nor the inequality update requires modification. We evaluated the construction on a Poisson benchmark, option-price surfaces, and air-quality fields. Under incomplete boundary data, a monotonicity constraint lowered the error of a two-dimensional Poisson reconstruction by $63\%$. On a real S&P 500 option chain thinned to $20$ retained quotes, no-arbitrage constraints reduced held-out extrapolation error by $22\%$ and cut the calendar-arbitrage rate from $10\%$ to $1\%$. On four real air-quality snapshots, nonnegativity reduced a Gaussian moment-based sub-zero-tail proxy by $74\%$ at the sparsest monitor count. Changes in mean accuracy and constraint diagnostics differed across data regimes. The probability of the noisy virtual constraint event under the equality posterior measures agreement with the specified bounds before the update. The construction turns admissibility from a post-processing step into conditioning information for probabilistic PDE solvers.

Amortized Counterfactual Prediction with Graph Neural Networks

Hendrik Stumpf, Francesco Quinzan

Counterfactual inference asks how an observed outcome might have differed under different circumstances, such as whether a patient would have recovered had they received a different treatment. Such questions arise whenever decisions depend on the outcomes of alternatives that were not observed. Answering counterfactual queries generally requires knowledge of the underlying causal structure, which is often unavailable in practice. A common approach is therefore to first estimate the causal graph from observational data and then perform counterfactual inference. However, errors in the estimated causal graph can propagate to downstream counterfactual predictions. To address this challenge, we introduce the Counterfactual Graph Neural Process (CFGNP), an end-to-end approach that answers counterfactual queries directly from observational data without first estimating a causal graph. CFGNP uses Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) to exploit the dual-graph structure by restricting cross-world message passing to corresponding factual--counterfactual variables. Across synthetic and image-based benchmarks, CFGNP achieves stronger predictive performance than existing approaches, including on the German Loan benchmark, where it reduces MSE from $4.51 \times 10^{-2}$ to $0.19 \times 10^{-2}$. Furthermore, on synthetic causal graphs, a model trained only on 10-node graphs maintains strong predictive performance on graphs with up to 30 nodes, without retraining.

X-AVDD: Cross-Attentive Audio-Visual Dataset Distillation

Saumyaranjan Mohanty, Aravind Reddy, Konda Reddy Mopuri

Audio-visual dataset distillation aims to synthesize a small dataset from a large paired audio-visual dataset, while nearly preserving the training utility for neural networks. Existing approaches such as DM and AVDD face two critical limitations: 1) they are memory-intensive, making them difficult to scale to complex architectures and higher samples-per-class (SPC) settings, and 2) they often fail to preserve strong cross-modal semantic correspondence. To tackle these issues, we propose X-AVDD, a cross-attention-based distillation method that explicitly captures inter-modal relationships during synthesis. By decoupling cross-modal alignment from joint optimization, X-AVDD is substantially more memory-efficient, reducing end-to-end synthesis cost by $\sim 8\times$ GPU-hours, while producing highly aligned synthetic data. Empirically, X-AVDD significantly outperforms other audio-visual distillation methods. On VGGSound, an audio-visual dataset of short YouTube clips, X-AVDD improves top-1 test accuracy from $8.2\%\rightarrow24.0\%$ at SPC=$10$ and $9.8\%\rightarrow26.5\%$ at SPC=$20$. Furthermore, X-AVDD improves cross-architecture generalization (e.g., $25.5\%\rightarrow35.3\%$ for a ViT target on AVE dataset at SPC $=50$) and successfully scales to complex architectures such as AudioCLIP, achieving $62.3\%$ accuracy using only $5.65\%$ of the full dataset, against the full dataset accuracy of $72.4\%$ while completing in $9\times$ lower training time compared to the full dataset.
