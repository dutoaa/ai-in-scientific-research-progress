AgentAM: An AI Agent for Spatter- and Defect-Aware Process Optimization in Laser Powder Bed Fusion

Olabode T. Ajenifujah, Amir Barati Farimani

Predictive models for laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) are rarely used on the shop floor because querying them requires programming and model-specific knowledge. We present AgentAM, a large language model (LLM) agent that gives process engineers natural-language access to CFD-trained surrogates of spatter and melt pool geometry for SS316L through the Model Context Protocol (MCP). Polynomial surrogates trained on 279 single-track simulations predict melt pool dimensions ($R^2 \geq 0.95$) and spatter volume ($R^2 = 0.83$ on test data) at any power-velocity point in the trained range, and geometric criteria assign each point a geometry-derived defect regime (keyhole, lack of fusion, balling or stable) that matches the regime obtained from the simulated geometry in 93.4% of tracks under cross-validation. The agent selects and parameterizes tools for prediction, process-window scans and constrained optimization. Minimum-spatter optima often sat within 10 $μ$m of the lack-of-fusion boundary; a margin-aware optimizer moved them away from all thresholds and raised the estimated probability of remaining classified Good from 0.94 to 1.00 under an assumed 2% process drift, and from 0.72 to 0.91 when jointly sampled cross-validated geometry residuals were added. About 40-50 well-spread simulations sufficed to reproduce the defect map. To evaluate the agent layer, we introduce a 145-prompt benchmark scored against direct tool calls, plus eight prompts (40 runs) on margin-aware requests. Over the 725 runs of the main benchmark the agent passed the intended inputs in 98.5% of parsing prompts, reported the scored spatter values and defect classes faithfully and met every stated constraint in the 65 feasible optimization runs, within a median 1.0% of the brute-force optimum.

Macrocanonical Generator Networks: Data-Efficient Neural Surrogates for Amortized Physics Simulation

Niall Jeffrey, Benjamin D. Wandelt

Many scientific simulations are computationally expensive, limiting their use in simulation-based inference, uncertainty quantification, and decision making. We introduce macrocanonical generator networks, a framework for learning fast, data-efficient neural surrogates for amortized simulation of stationary physical processes by matching multiscale statistics of a target process. Inspired by microcanonical maximum-entropy synthesis, which matches feature statistics per sample by inner-loop optimization, our approach instead trains a neural generator to satisfy the same constraints in expectation, preserving physically realistic sample-to-sample fluctuations. We prove that gradient-descent training induces preconditioned gradient-descent dynamics in sample space that inherit the symmetry-preservation properties of the microcanonical gradient descent of Bruna and Mallat, amortizing its per-sample inner-loop optimization into a single training run after which every sample is generated in one forward pass. We further show that residual generator parameterizations initialized near the identity, trained with a small learning rate, preserve entropy locally during early optimization and mitigate mode collapse. Experiments on cosmology and fluid simulations show that macrocanonical generators recover realistic samples from extremely small training sets, in some cases a single training example, while generating new realizations more than $10^6$ times faster per sample than the reference simulator and more than $10^3$ times faster than microcanonical gradient descent. The trained generators can be reused for downstream pretraining, including simulation-based inference, providing a data-efficient route to multi-fidelity scientific machine learning workflows.

Multi-Objective Generative Optimization via Expected Hypervolume Fine-Tuning

Juan Guevara, Riccardo De Santi, Andreas Krause, Raul Astudillo

Scientific discovery requires plausible designs spanning useful trade-offs among competing properties: a molecule with strong binding affinity, for example, may be difficult to synthesize. Pretrained generative models, e.g., flow and diffusion generators, provide a foundation for proposing such designs, but steering them toward high-quality, complementary trade-offs remains challenging. Inference-time search incurs high per-sample costs, while fine-tuning typically relies on fixed per-candidate rewards that do not capture how designs complement one another. We introduce Hypervolume Fine-Tuning (HVFT), which adapts a single pretrained generator to maximize the expected hypervolume of its samples while controlling departure from the pretrained distribution. Expected hypervolume jointly rewards candidate quality and collective trade-off coverage. Nonetheless, we show that it cannot be expressed as the expectation of a fixed per-candidate reward and therefore cannot be optimized directly with standard techniques. A key observation of this work is that its first variation corresponds to the expected marginal hypervolume contribution of a candidate, which yields a tractable adaptive reward via an unbiased estimator based on model samples. HVFT uses this reward to decompose the objective into a sequence of KL-proximal scalar-reward subproblems. We establish concavity, relative smoothness, and convergence guarantees for the resulting distributional procedure. Across therapeutic-peptide and molecular design tasks spanning discrete diffusion and continuous flow-matching models, HVFT achieves state-of-the-art expected hypervolume and trade-off coverage.

Together, our results show that improving feedback fidelity is the key to substantially advancing interactive HTML generation.

Adversarial Speculative Reasoning Improves Long Horizon Agents

Hongyin Luo, Wei Fang, Lijie Yang, Owen Stepan, Daniel Suit, Jeff Krapf

Speculative decoding has been treated purely as a systems optimization that speeds up large language model decoding without changing the model behavior. Under this view, the growing effort to train better draft models has been focused solely on acceptance rate. We argue that this view does not hold true for long-horizon agent reasoning. Measuring frontier open-weight models on complex agentic tasks with different speculative decoding approaches, we found that numerical noise grows within verification windows, and the draft acceptance results of the thinking process can change the output distribution of the model after 30 steps, thus noticeably influencing the reasoning accuracy of long-horizon agents in random directions. In this work, we improve the reasoning capability of target models by controlling such noise. While we cannot change the overall token distribution verified by the target model, we can encode critical reasoning with highest precision by training the draft model to be rejected. We identify these points in agent traces and train the draft model to favor adversarial preference. At inference the trained draft and the target part ways precisely at these decision points, verification restarts, so that critical thinking tokens are sampled from the target model and encoded under its least noisy conditions. This approach makes critical reasoning better indexed and more durable over a long horizon. On complex agentic coding benchmarks, our approach raises end-to-end success rate with no change to the target model or its output distribution. Our results recast the speculative decoding mechanism from a pure accelerator into an active allocator for context precision that improves the accuracy of long-horizon agents by training 0.06% to 0.28% of hosted parameters.

Such agents must jointly infer high-level plans, navigate through intermediate states, track progress over extended interaction histories, and execute visually grounded actions. Although prior work has explored modular multi-agent systems with a separate planner, the planner is typically not updated jointly with the actor. We investigate whether explicitly separating and jointly training planning and execution provides a stronger inductive bias for long-horizon web navigation. We introduce JHiMA-WebRL, a joint hierarchical multi-agent reinforcement learning framework for web agents that couples a task decomposer with an actor. To train the decomposer, we use a strong teacher model to construct structured subtask supervision from multimodal GUI trajectories, and use these annotations to fine-tune both the decomposer and the subtask-conditioned actor. Ablations show that jointly updating the decomposer during RL outperforms updating the actor alone. These results suggest that jointly learned role factorization with smaller models can be a competitive alternative to scaling monolithic agents for long-horizon web tasks.

Learning curves in natural and artificial systems

Dmitry A. Novikov

A general mathematical model of experience formation is considered, in which the learning level is defined as the probability that the next value of the uncertainty factor has already been mastered and not forgotten. Within this model the shape of the learning curve is derived, and it turns out to be determined primarily by the distribution of situation occurrence: a uniform distribution yields an exponential curve, whereas a power-law distribution yields a power-law curve whose exponent equals the ratio of the distribution exponent to that same exponent increased by one. Both mechanisms generating power-law curves, established earlier in the analysis of debugging, assembly and repair activities, are shown to have been independently rediscovered in deep learning theory as the "frequency" and "spectral" mechanisms of scaling laws for artificial neural networks, while the exponential curves of machine learning (contraction operators, strongly convex optimization, stationary reinforcement) are generated by the same constancy of the contraction coefficient that was revealed half a century earlier in models of radioactive decay, the first-order lag element, and others. A solution is given for the problem of choosing a training distribution that differs from the deployment distribution. A methodological consequence is discussed: three distinct mechanisms generate curves indistinguishable in shape, so an observed learning curve does not allow one to judge the "internal" structure of the learning system.

Aligning Language Models with General Preferences: An Empirical Study of Direct Nash Optimization

Austin Tsai, Saurav Nanda, Ganapathy Parthasarathy, Bhuvnesh Kumar, Parivesh Choudhary

Direct Nash Optimization (DNO) aligns a language model to a general preference function by approximating the Nash equilibrium of a two-player preference game. Its practical variant, DNO-Prct, iterates on-policy sampling, LLM-based rating of the samples together with a teacher's gold response, large-margin preference-pair selection and a DPO-style contrastive update. We re-implement DNO-Prct independently and study it on two open-weight instruct models, Llama 3.1 8B Instruct and Mistral 7B Instruct v0.1, with GPT-4o or a larger open-weight model of the same family as the annotator. On AlpacaEval 2.0 with GPT-4o as judge, three iterations raise the length-controlled (LC) win rate against GPT-4o from 31.7% to 45.3% for Llama and from 6.8% to 18.4% for Mistral, and the tuned 8B model scores above Llama 3.1 70B Instruct on every reported win rate. With same-family open-weight annotators, the runs retain 62% (Llama) and 82% (Mistral) of the LC gain achieved with GPT-4o as annotator, so the rating step does not require a proprietary model. In a separate ablation configuration, variants that remove the gold responses, remove the data look-back, or accept only teacher-preferred pairs score 6 to 10 LC points below the full procedure, whereas varying the number of on-policy samples per prompt (1 to 7) moves the LC win rate by at most 2.1 points, and a margin sweep (1 to 5) run in its own pipeline by at most 2.4 points. We also report the cost of the pipeline and where the practical algorithm departs from the theory it approximates. All results are single runs, and GPT-4o is both the teacher and the evaluation judge; we discuss what this implies for the reported gains.

De Rham cohomological architectures for PINNs and PDEs with topological holes

Andrew Gracyk

We attempt to alleviate failure modes of classical physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) in scenarios of topological pathologies corresponding to punctures, holes, poles, and voids by introducing de Rham cohomological augmentations, or Hodge-de Rham PINNs. Vanilla PINNs often encounter pitfalls in hole-based settings due to discontinuous interfaces, nonzero closed path integration, path dependence, correspondence to exact forms via the exterior derivative, etc., therefore we absolve traditional failures via parameterizing a 1-form by the Hodge-Morrey-Friedrichs (HMF) decomposition for manifolds with boundary, drawing a connection to de Rham cohomology such as through the HMF isomorphism theorem. 1-forms can act as representations of scalar-valued PDE solutions through integral operator mappings. We propose performing a PINN-based loss on the parameterized 1-form through an associated PDE, which subsequently solves the potential-based PINN problem and moreover remedies the failures of vanilla PINNs via the Sobolev-regular operator under a Fréchet differentiation interconnection. The operator relationship often has a nontrivial closed-form and often requires integration or a case-by-case analysis, which we address thoroughly in our experiments. When an integrability condition is not met on our losses, we can equip exhaustion functions to train the networks. We demonstrate our methods on the Aharonov-Bohm effect, which, to our understanding, is largely nonexistent in PINN literature. We develop theoretical guarantees of our methods and demonstrate our pathological safeguards on a broad selection of topologically-adverse problems, including scenarios of inexact forms, and those of nontrivial harmonics and coexact terms.

Following RL optimization, iGC maintains SHM sequence structure. These results demonstrate that iGC integrates biologically plausible mutation generation with antigen-specific affinity selection, providing an in silico framework for iterative antibody affinity maturation.

Offline methods can learn from completed trajectories, but deploy one shared harness that may overfit training tasks and cannot react to evidence revealed during a new run.
