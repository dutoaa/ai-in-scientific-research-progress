ServerBench: How can your coding agents design specialized LLM serving systems?

Xinrui Zhong, Zhiming Wang, Yongqi Liu, Zhaohui Wang, Shanglin Yang, Zikun Li

Large language model (LLM) coding agents develop software through experimentation, offering an opportunity to design the serving systems supporting their own operation. Effective serving requires coordinating implementation and operating decisions under session dependencies, latency constraints, and correctness requirements. We introduce ServerBench, a benchmark for agentic design of complete, specialized LLM serving systems. Agents experiment against a common objective, dollars per dollar: latency-qualified token value per dollar of GPU cost. Stateful replay preserves session dependencies, and independent verification checks the submitted system. Across 11 coding-agent models and three deployments, Claude Sonnet 5.5 achieves 2.97 dollars per dollar on Gemma E4B, $4.87\times$ the vLLM reference; 22 of 33 runs yield verified systems, and 13 exceed the strongest baseline. Our analysis identifies three findings: high-value designs coordinate capacity improvements with load selection; agents interpret performance feedback through workload-relevant experiments; and reliable delivery requires verification of the execution paths introduced by optimization. Overall, ServerBench supports systematic study of how agents translate service objectives and feedback into effective, verified serving systems for given task specifications.

Energy-Guided Transport for Projection-Free Physics-Informed Flow Matching

Giuliano Galadini, Aurelio Uncini, Danilo Comminiello

Deep generative models, particularly Flow Matching frameworks, have emerged as powerful tools for synthesizing complex physical systems. However, standard architectures remain purely statistically driven and agnostic to the underlying governing equations, often violating fundamental physical constraints during inference. Existing zero-shot approaches designed to enforce these constraints typically rely on rigid algebraic projections. While effective for sparse boundary conditions, applying such discrete geometric corrections to dense, full-domain PDEs may disrupt the probability path, \rev{potentially deviating from the learned manifold and introducing substantial computational overhead}. To address these challenges, we introduce Energy-Guided Transport, a novel training-free framework that formulates non-linear physical constraints as a smooth potential energy landscape. Rather than interrupting the ODE integration, our method applies a continuous dissipative force directly to the intermediate generative states, steering them toward the physical manifold. By evaluating this force implicitly via reverse-mode automatic differentiation, our framework preserves the natural dynamics of the flow while maintaining an efficient $\mathcal{O}(N)$ algorithmic complexity. We extensively evaluate our framework across various 1D and 2D physical systems, including the chaotic Navier-Stokes equations. Our method achieves stable generation without structural divergence, significantly improves the stability-accuracy trade-off compared to state-of-the-art methods, and substantially accelerates inference with respect to exact projection strategies. Code available at: https://github.com/ispamm/Projection-free-energy-guided-flow-matching.

PINR-ESI: Physics-Informed Self-Supervised Implicit Neural Representation Framework for EEG Extended-Source Imaging

Zhiyong Zhou, Sheng Liao, Youshen Xiao, Xiaolin Li, Yakang Dai

EEG extended-source imaging aims to localize active cortical regions and characterize their spatial extent, yet supervised inverse networks typically depend on synthetic EEG-source pairs and may be sensitive to synthetic-to-real mismatch. We propose a physics-informed self-supervised implicit neural representation (INR) framework for spatial source reconstruction without paired source labels or simulation-based supervised pretraining. The lead field matrix encodes the source-to-sensor volume-conduction model and constrains the INR through agreement between predicted and measured EEG. Cortical current density is modeled as a continuous coordinate-conditioned field. A classical inverse estimate provides warm initialization and spatial constraints on source activity, while patch-integrated projection maps the field to discrete source coefficients for forward modeling. Simulations with varying noise levels, source counts, and source extents showed the clearest advantage in identifying active cortical regions, particularly for multiple and larger sources. Quantitative and qualitative evaluations using real EEG recordings from epilepsy patients and intracranial stimulation experiments further supported the feasibility of the framework for source imaging. These findings suggest the potential of physics-informed self-supervised INR for extended-source imaging.

A Framework for Context Enrichment and Selective Robustness in LLM Social Agents

Mattia Greiche, Reihaneh Rabbany, Maximilian Puelma Touzel

Researchers use large language models as agents in social simulations, where agents are prompted with observations and background information intended to shape their actions. Yet LLM agents have been shown to substantially change their behavior under meaning-preserving prompt variations, creating a validation problem for social simulation. One plausible hypothesis is that this brittleness reflects a lack of grounding and can be reduced by enriching the context provided to the agents. Whether richer context improves robustness remains unclear, in part because context richness and robustness are rarely defined in ways that support controlled comparisons. We address the first need with controlled context enrichment, a set of prompt substitution rules that systematically enriches distinct types of context: neutral padding, generic social background, descriptive decision grounding, and normative decision grounding. We address the second by introducing selective robustness as a validation criterion: insensitivity to meaning-preserving prompt variation combined with a directional response to decision-relevant changes in the social world. We demonstrate the framework in a Prisoner's Dilemma-like game, showing how it can be used to identify which forms of context enrichment produce more desirable and robust agent behavior. We find that these forms of enrichment produce distinct behavioral patterns that vary between the two models tested.

BEAKER: An Expert-Curated Benchmark for Embodied Brains in Self-Driving Chemical Laboratories

Fei Lin, Tengchao Zhang, Ziyang Gong, Xiaotong Yu, Bohan Zhang, Yifan Zhou

Self-Driving Chemical Laboratories (SDCLs) are moving chemical experimentation toward embodied automation, where machines must perceive laboratory scenes, reason about experimental states, and act under physical, procedural, and safety constraints. Although Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are increasingly considered as embodied brains for such systems, existing laboratory benchmarks mainly focus on safety, anomaly detection, or general scientific understanding, leaving their execution-oriented embodied capabilities underexplored. To fill this gap, we introduce BEAKER, a Benchmark for evaluating Embodied Actionability, Knowledge, Experimentation, and Reasoning in SDCLs. BEAKER contains 1,000 expert-curated question-answer samples across 11 image and video task types, covering key judgments required for chemical laboratory execution, including affordance grounding, trajectory planning, equipment, state, and safety recognition, apparatus setup, operation and step reasoning, and temporal procedure reasoning. All questions and annotations are manually created and reviewed by chemistry and embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI) experts, without AI-assisted generation or annotation. We evaluate 28 general-purpose, science-oriented, chemistry-specialized, and embodied MLLMs in an offline, single-turn setting. Results show that current models can follow coarse experimental procedures in videos, but still struggle to ground actions into operable regions and feasible trajectories, to recognize fine-grained laboratory apparatus, states, and safety norms, and to order steps in mid-length procedures. BEAKER thus reveals a clear gap between coarse procedural understanding and the precise, action-grounded perception required for laboratory execution, providing a diagnostic benchmark for future MLLMs in SDCLs.

SecureGARD: Dynamic Ontology-Guided Governance for Secure Tool-Using LLM Agents

Guoqin Xiong, Shengtao Wen, Xiang Chen, Xinyi Zeng, Zhaoxin Fan, Wei Ye

Tool-using large language model (LLM) agents translate model outputs into consequential actions, turning indirect prompt injection into an execution-security problem. Existing defenses constrain tool calls, trajectories, or information flows, but often leave action semantics, state dependencies, and evidence relations implicit, limiting generalization across heterogeneous tools and complicating safe updates. We present \ours{}, a dynamic ontology-guided governance framework. \ours{} grounds heterogeneous calls in a shared semantic representation and combines an executable ontology with episode-level task state to derive deterministic ready, waiting, and forbidden decisions. A waiting decision identifies an unmet but recoverable obligation, enabling bounded replanning without expanding the agent's authorization boundary. \ours{} evolves its ontology offline from verified training executions. An offline LLM proposes instance-independent action--state axioms, which are accepted only if they address ontology errors exposed by training failures, preserve logical consistency and protected entailments, and pass held-out validation before the ontology is frozen. We evaluate \ours{} on AgentDojo, ASB, and AgentDyn under benign and indirect prompt-injection settings. \ours{} achieves a 0\% attack success rate (ASR) across all three benchmarks while improving task utility under attack compared with existing baselines. These results show that explicit semantic governance can strengthen execution security without indiscriminate blocking.

Where It Breaks: Diagnosing Perception, Memory, and Decision Failures of a Vision-Language Agent in Pac-Man

Xinglu Wang, Edward Wang, Yong Zhang, Zhenan Fan

Training an agent to play Pac-Man with only up, down, left and right actions raises a basic question: when performance is poor, what should be improved? Difficulty can arise from reading the screen or from choosing actions over a long sequence. We examine these problems in a Qwen3.5-9B-based agent through separate perception checks and primitive-policy reinforcement-learning studies. In the policy study, which uses textual boards to remove image transcription from the decision interface, completion on eight held-out mazes increased from 0% before this optimization to 80.2% at a selected checkpoint, with three extra lives. The perception checks show that accurate static screenshot reading can coexist with errors during interaction. A separate policy comparison also shows that further optimization can accompany worse action choices. Together, these findings motivate concrete checks for post-training: evaluate perception in use, distinguish training success from full-game validation, and examine decisions and later performance rather than relying on one selected score. The study provides empirical lessons for improving future agent training and evaluation.

Adaptive Multi Mode: Budget-Aware Routing of Optimizers By Online Landscape Probes

Benjamin Jacob Bodner

The best classical optimizer for a continuous black-box problem depends on its budget and landscape. Existing routers decide from the dimension and budget, past improvement or features fitted to one benchmark, and few test an optimizer's implicit assumptions online. AMM (Adaptive Multi Mode) routes a known budget among quasi-Newton, trust-region, coordinate, multistart and evolution-strategy optimization engines with online probes of curvature, plateaus, separability, kinks, basins and fractal structure. Each probe has a default, a dimension-by-budget validity range and a cost charged to the budget. In its pre-registered development loop on bbob, 32 of 36 predictions held over the last 16 confirmations. AMM was then tested once on held-out bbob instances 101-110 (D=2-40, 10*D-1000*D) against up to seven competitors. It has the highest target share in 382 of 600 function-dimension-budget cells (236 outright, 146 tied) and, by a descriptive posterior rule, is credibly behind in 35, mostly at 10*D and 30*D. At every budget it also leads the controls built from its own engines (the best engine alone, the best engine per dimension and budget, and a greedy schedule), narrowly at 10*D and 30*D, and nevergrad's NgIohTuned. In pre-registered runs its lead over IPOP-CMA-ES holds under affine and box transformations, on box-constrained bbob and, pooled, on rotated f2-f4, but rotating the separable f3 and f4 removes most of the lead there. On held-out MA-BBOB its final share at 2000*D is level with IPOP-CMA-ES's and its anytime area lower. We published AMM as an open-source Python package (pip install amm-optimizer; https://github.com/BjBodner/amm-optimizer).

Thermally Conditioned Time-Series Forecasting of Grain Growth in Polycrystalline Materials

Eliane Younes, Elie Hachem, Marc Bernacki

Grain growth during heat treatment strongly affects the microstructure of polycrystalline materials and, consequently, their mechanical properties. Although numerical methods such as front-tracking or front-capturing simulations can accurately describe grain growth, their high computational cost limits their use for long or non-isothermal thermal cycles. In previous work, grain growth was successfully predicted under isothermal conditions by treating grain size distributions as time series and forecasting their evolution using recurrent neural networks, allowing complete grain growth trajectories to be generated in seconds rather than through costly PDE-based simulations. In the present work, this forecasting framework is reformulated to handle non-isothermal conditions by explicitly incorporating thermal information into the model architecture. Grain size distributions are encoded using a convolutional neural network, thermal descriptors are embedded through a dedicated multilayer perceptron, and temporal evolution is predicted using a recurrent network. Instantaneous temperature and thermal rate are provided at each time step, allowing the model to adapt its predictions to changing thermal conditions. The framework is trained and evaluated on datasets generated from validated front-tracking simulations across a wide range of non-isothermal thermal paths. The results show that the thermally conditioned model accurately reproduces grain growth dynamics under complex thermal histories, achieving, in k-fold cross-validation over all simulations, mean relative errors of 2.99% (one-hour cycles) and 4.51% (four-hour cycles) with coefficients of determination exceeding 0.99, and mean relative errors between 1.91% and 14.66% on ten simulations following four unseen thermal histories of increasing complexity.

From Reconstruction to Imputation: Test-Time Optimization for Missing Time-Series Channels

Björn Ludwig, Alexander Windmann, René Heesch, Oliver Niggemann

Complete sensor-channel failures create a difficult form of time-series missingness: local interpolation is unavailable, and deploying a separately trained imputer for every failure pattern may be impractical. We ask whether a sequence reconstructor trained without artificial mask augmentation and then frozen can be converted into an imputer at inference time. Our method treats the missing entries of each test window as optimization variables, clamps all observations, and backpropagates through the frozen model to minimize reconstruction error only on observed coordinates. The resulting observed-self-consistency objective requires neither artificial mask augmentation nor full-channel dropout during reconstructor training. We characterize when this refinement is informative through the cross-Jacobian from missing inputs to observed residuals, and use an optional temporal-smoothness prior when that coupling is weak. Across five real-world datasets with exactly one channel synthetically masked per test window and four reconstruction architectures, no-prior test-time refinement reduces MAE relative to one-pass reconstruction in 19 of 20 dataset-backbone combinations. Compared with mask-aware imputers trained under either standard structured masks or matched full-channel dropout, the best refined reconstructor achieves the lowest MAE on two datasets and trails the strongest baseline on three. These gains require substantially more inference time and are sensitive to cross-channel coupling. Our results establish test-time self-consistency as a conditional inference-time adapter for frozen reconstruction models that complements mask-aware imputation without retraining the reconstructor.

The Behavioral Rate of Fine-Tuning: Compression as a Measure of Useful Information

Joey David, Paul Caillon, Alexandre Allauzen

Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) adapts a frozen language model through a compact learned update, making it a natural setting for asking how much information fine-tuning needs to write. Yet adapter rank, parameter count, or numerical reconstruction error do not directly tell us how many bits are required to preserve the acquired behavior. We therefore define an operational behavioral rate $R^\star$ as the smallest serialized size of a compressed adapter that, once reloaded into the frozen base model, retains a fixed fraction of its held-out fine-tuning gain. Across LoRA fine-tuning experiments, this rate increases with distinct training content at fixed optimization budgets, but is not determined by intuitive metrics like source description length, fine-tuning gain, or weight-space reconstruction error. Most importantly, the same datasets exhibit substantially different, and even reordered, rates across frozen base models, showing that fine-tuning compressibility is a property of the dataset--receiver pair rather than of the dataset alone. However, we show that correction spectra measured on the data are able to rank these rates within a receiver. Finally, we study a striking noise-reducing effect of compression: adapters trained on deliberately corrupted datasets fall far below the frozen model, yet after compression score above it (23.7% to 82.9% on GSM8K, against 77.6% for frozen Qwen2.5-7B), consistently across tested families and sizes. The damage is unevenly concentrated in the adapter's leading singular directions, which compression appears to filter. Away from its training task, the compressed corrupted adapter also generalizes better than a clean fine-tune: on harder math it retains 97% of base accuracy, versus 44% for the clean adapter.
