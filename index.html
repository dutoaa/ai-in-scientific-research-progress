Sage-Taiyi: A Metacognitive Architecture for Scientific Discovery

Weida Wang, Benteng Chen, Yigeng Jiang, Shuchen Pu, Weiping Shen, Zichao Liu

We introduce Sage-Taiyi, a metacognition-inspired architecture that uses accumulated scientific understanding to guide what to investigate next and how to investigate it. Through explicit monitoring and control, the system revises its understanding of evidence, assumptions, and unresolved questions, then uses these assessments to adapt research targets and select reusable reasoning methods. On Taiyi-Bench, an AI co-scientist ideation benchmark comprising 265 tasks from 50 papers across ten disciplines, Sage-Taiyi ranks first on all eight metrics of historical rediscovery and open-ended ideation, achieving an Overall score of 54.0 compared with 47.9 for the strongest baseline, AI Scientist-v2. It also performs well on two external scientific ideation benchmarks. Five expert-guided collaborations further connect its proposals to experimental and computational validation across chemistry, materials science, and engineering. In chemistry, reaction-condition optimization raises the yield of cobalt-catalyzed carbonylation from trace amounts to 84% and photocatalytic allylic amination from 13% to 69%. These results support metacognitive regulation as an approach to scientific ideation and its progression toward practical validation.

Shortcuts and Boundaries: Verifiable RL Misalignment and Contextual Safety Learning

Xinjie Shen

Reinforcement learning (RL) can improve an agent's task capability while reinforcing behavior that violates contextual execution requirements. We study this reward-driven misalignment, including reward hacking, through verifiable reward--context conflicts, where compliant task completion remains feasible while some executable shortcuts earn more reward than every compliant strategy. Encoding these conflicts in task dynamics lets us generate diverse interactive environments at scale and derive compliance feedback from execution histories. In resource and reporting workflows, task optimization increases real productivity through unauthorized resource use or raises reported scores while actual work declines. Controlled changes in the training distribution show that minority conflict exposure can still lead to widespread shortcut acquisition, reaching 97.9\% certified profitable violations on an API task under a 25\% conflict mixture after 128 updates. The same contextual checks supply a simple binary penalty that supports joint capability and compliance gains and partially mitigates acquired violations. Learned safety adapts to expanded authorization and transfers across mechanisms into workflows with evolving evidence and approval requirements. On 32 unseen changing-evidence tasks, resource-trained safety increases safe completion from 53.1\% to 89.1\% relative to task-only training, while both policies achieve full real work. These results connect a verifiable account of reward-driven misalignment with scalable task construction and safety learning that remains useful under changing execution requirements.

Safe Reinforcement Learning for Multi-Turn Clinical Agents: When Dense Rewards Beat Outcome Rewards

Brendan P. Murphy, Agustina Saenz

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards is increasingly used to improve language model reasoning, yet its effects on sequential decision procedures remain poorly understood. We study this problem in clinical multi-turn agents that must decide what information to request, when to stop gathering evidence, and which action to take. We introduce three controlled environments: medication refill triage, acute diagnostic work-up, and dosing titration, each with hidden clinical states, query costs, exact adaptive oracles, and deterministic outcome verifiers. Across these tasks, outcome-only group relative policy optimization often improves aggregate reward while producing undesirable procedures, including premature commitment, one-question collapse, and failures on rare high-risk cases. In diagnostics, assigning dense credit to questions that reduce uncertainty improves held-out value and moderate-case support across three training seeds relative to outcome-only reinforcement learning, without the high-risk seed collapse observed under outcome-only training. Separating exploration from exploitation further increases high-risk support at its best checkpoint, but at the cost of excessive information gathering on low-acuity cases. Additional experiments show that the optimal strength of process credit is task-dependent; that after a supervised warmup that already gathers carefully, reinforcement learning raises aggregate reward by trimming questions while degrading high-risk performance; and that reinforcement learning does not reliably repair rare behavior gaps inherited from biased supervised data. Verifiable final outcomes are therefore insufficient for training reliable multi-turn agents: reward design must account for the procedure used to reach an answer, and evaluation must distinguish adaptive information gathering from reward-exploiting shortcuts.

Kolmogorov-Chapman Maps: Trajectory-Free Learning for Simulating Molecular Dynamics

Majdi Hassan, Marta Skreta, Dominique Beaini, Kirill Neklyudov

Stochastic differential equations (SDEs) describe stochastic processes across a wide range of scientific domains, including physics, biology, and finance. Although individual numerical integration steps are inexpensive, observing meaningful events or transitions can require millions of sequential steps, resulting in substantial wall-clock simulation time and poor hardware utilization. Deep learning offers an alternative by amortizing many integration steps into a single neural network forward pass, potentially enabling much faster simulation. However, existing supervised approaches typically require long simulated trajectory data, which restricts their applicability. In this work, we introduce Kolmogorov-Chapman Maps (KC-Maps), a method for learning the transition kernel of an SDE that relies only on the trajectory-free marginal samples together with the known drift and diffusion coefficients. Given an initial time and state of a process, KC-Maps models its distribution integrated to any given time, corresponding to the weak solution of the SDE. To recover the correct dynamics, KC-Maps satisfies two key properties: (i) it satisfies the boundary conditions, which we incorporate directly into the neural network architecture, and (ii) it respects the Kolmogorov--Chapman equation, which provides an efficient training objective. We empirically demonstrate that KC-Maps learns to integrate the dynamics of peptide molecular systems and outperforms classical molecular dynamics in terms of GPU-hours.

CoilStellaration: A Dataset and Benchmark for Engineering-Aware Stellarator Coilset Generation

Jan-Hendrik Ewers, Santiago A. Cadena, Nicolò Foppiani, Andrea Merlo

Stellarators are fusion devices that use externally generated, three-dimensional magnetic fields to confine hot plasma and offer a promising route to commercial fusion. Their electromagnetic coils pose a challenging design problem: they must reproduce the target magnetic field while satisfying engineering constraints and remaining practical and cost-effective to manufacture. Finding suitable geometries is a highly nonlinear inverse problem that generally has neither a closed-form nor a unique solution. This problem lies at the heart of stellarator design. We introduce CoilStellaration, a dataset and benchmark for learning the mapping between coil geometries from target plasma magnetic fields and engineering requirements. The dataset contains 177,000 optimized filamentary coilsets targeting plasma equilibria from ConStellaration, with sampled engineering requirements and achieved geometric and magnetic-field metrics. It spans multiple coil counts and field-period numbers, supporting studies of both specialized and shared models across configurations. The benchmark asks models to predict coils conditioned on a target equilibrium and requirements on coil-coil and coil-plasma spacing, curvature, and magnetic-field error. We show that magnetic-field accuracy remains the main challenge, with learned models rarely satisfying all requirements simultaneously. However, using learned predictions to initialize conventional coil optimization can accelerate early progress. We release baseline models and evaluation code to support reproducible research on both direct coil generation and learned optimization initializations. By lowering the barrier for machine learning and optimization researchers to engage with stellarator coil design, we aim to accelerate progress toward practical, cost-effective stellarator power plants.

Agentic-OPD: Recovering Forgotten Agent Capabilities via On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Tasks

Tingcheng Bian, Kangye Gong, Jinchang Luo, MingQuan Cheng, Feiran Liu, Ni Li

Reinforcement learning is now the mainstream paradigm for training LLMs on agentic tasks, yet standard practice keeps only the best-validation checkpoint and discards the rest. We show that this is lossy: in GRPO training of LLM agents, the best-validation checkpoint fails on problems that earlier checkpoints from the same run had already solved, and the union of problems solved across checkpoints far exceeds any single one. The discarded checkpoints thus form a teacher pool obtained at no additional training cost, which on-policy distillation (OPD) can mine to recover the lost coverage. Mining this pool raises two challenges: i) which checkpoint to distill from, as no checkpoint dominates the others and complementarity to the student is problem-specific; ii) where to trust it, as even a well-chosen teacher is only locally superior, so indiscriminate distillation overwrites behavior the student has already mastered. We propose Agentic-OPD, which treats the best-validation checkpoint as the student and all saved checkpoints as frozen teacher candidates, using the student's evolving capability gaps to guide both teacher selection and supervision filtering: the HCN policy (Highest Coverage Next) dynamically selects teachers by weighted coverage of the student's unsolved problems, while the LAD module (Locality-Aware Distillation) confines transfer to failed student rollouts on problems where the teacher performs better. Across five agentic task domains and 24 benchmarks and task subsets, Agentic-OPD improves over its initialization checkpoint by up to 7.4 points, with a 4B student surpassing the latest frontier models GPT-5.6 and DeepSeek-V4.1 on deep search. Agentic-OPD recasts a finished RL run as a reusable pool of policies and establishes post-RL capability recovery as a stage orthogonal to improvements in the RL algorithm itself.

Conventional palpation is subjective and labor-intensive, while many non-destructive methods require specialized equipment or substantial computational resources. This study developed a smartphone-based transillumination and 3D point-cloud framework for non-destructive WB severity classification. A total of 267 chicken breast fillets, including normal breast (NB, n = 87), mild WB (MWB, n = 85), and severe WB (SWB, n = 95), were evaluated using RAW transillumination images and 3D point clouds acquired with an iPhone 16 Pro. The 2D-only models achieved approximately 82% test accuracy, while the multimodal ensemble combining Fusion-DGCNN, XGBoost, and physics-guided logistic regression (PGLR) achieved approximately 90% accuracy. These findings demonstrate the complementary value of optical, geometric, and physics-informed features and establish the feasibility of smartphone-based, non-destructive WB severity assessment.

VLA Adaptation with Real and Simulated Data: A Case Study on Robotic Chess

Matěj Kripner, Milan Straka

Vision-language-action (VLA) models are trained as general-purpose foundation models for robot learning, and require fine-tuning for each downstream task and robot. This raises practical questions such as how much data and training updates are needed, how checkpoints should be selected, and whether simulation can be leveraged to alleviate the cost of real data collection. We study these questions on a task that requires both precise manipulation and spatial understanding of the scene. In this task, an SO-101 arm moves a chess king between two squares that are given only by their names, such as e4 and a5. We fine-tune $π_{0.5}$ with various dataset sizes and update budgets, using teleoperated demonstrations as well as simulated demonstrations from a randomized scripted expert that imitates the variability of human demonstrations. In simulation, the success rate scales with both data and updates. Importantly, we find that a short learning-rate cool-down adds up to 31 points and is necessary for comparing checkpoints, and that validation loss and action error are unreliable for model selection. On the real arm, we fine-tune on up to 256 teleoperated demonstrations and evaluate ten policies in 25 trials each. Partial results indicate that pretraining on the simulated demonstrations helps substantially, most clearly when real data is scarce. We also present a negative result, in which training on the policy's own rollouts with filtered behavior cloning, RECAP-style advantage conditioning, corrections from an automatic coach, or residual reinforcement learning did not yield a measurable improvement over plain behavior cloning in our setting. We release the simulator, the expert and the real demonstrations.

TRACE: Adaptive Trust-Region Bayesian Optimization for Open-Ended Molecular Design

Madhav R. Muthyala, Mahsa Askari, Farshud Sorourifar, Evan Zhou, Joe Gauthier, Joel A. Paulson

Optimizing molecules under small evaluation budgets requires deciding both where in chemical space to search and which candidate within that region to evaluate. We introduce Trust Region Adaptive Chemical Exploration (TRACE), a framework for open-ended molecular Bayesian optimization (BO), where candidates are generated on demand rather than selected from a fixed library. TRACE combines an objective-free graph-perturbation mechanism with a fixed, chemistry-aware Gaussian process (GP) surrogate using fragprints, a representation combining molecular fingerprints and fragment descriptors. Rather than learning the search geometry itself, TRACE makes the scale of chemical exploration explicit through a Tanimoto-similarity threshold controlling chemical extent and a cardinality budget controlling candidate-search breadth. A simple success/failure controller expands or contracts both quantities during optimization, while the GP and acquisition function select which admissible molecules to evaluate. Across six challenging molecular optimization benchmarks under matched 1,000-evaluation budgets, TRACE achieves the strongest overall sample efficiency as measured by the best molecule found as evaluations accumulate. Crossed ablations on two objectives further show that adaptive region control contributes beyond the proposal mechanism, while its benefit depends on the proposal distribution. We further apply TRACE to the stability of iron phthalocyanine (FePc), a non-precious molecular electrocatalyst whose degradation can involve loss of the Fe center. Using a first-principles density functional theory (DFT) workflow designed for accurate energy evaluation, TRACE increases the computed thermodynamic resistance to Fe dissolution from 0.42 to 2.59 eV over 80 designed evaluations.

PORT: Preference Optimization via Robust Token-Level Reweighting

Ding Zhu, Xiukun Wei, Tian Xie, Zhihui Zhu, Xueru Zhang, Mohammad Mahdi Khalili

Preference optimization has become a central approach for aligning large language models with human values, but its effectiveness depends heavily on the quality of preference annotations. In practice, preference data is often noisy due to annotation errors and ambiguity. Existing robust preference optimization methods primarily operate at the sequence level, implicitly treating entire responses as uniformly correct or incorrect. However, rejected responses may still contain informative reasoning steps, while preferred responses can include subtle errors. In this work, we propose \textbf{P}reference \textbf{O}ptimization via \textbf{R}obust \textbf{T}oken-level reweighting \textbf{(PORT)}, a fine-grained framework for robust alignment under noisy preferences. PORT performs token-level reweighting using the empirical cumulative distribution function (CDF) of token log-probabilities, yielding an efficient forward-pass-only proxy to gradient-norm penalization that selectively suppresses corrupted tokens without additional backward-pass computation. We provide theoretical analysis showing that PORT reduces the gradient bias from noisy preference labels by minimizing an upper bound on the bias term. Extensive experiments across diverse noise settings demonstrate that PORT consistently improves robustness and outperforms existing sequence-level baselines. The code is available at https://github.com/osu-srml/PORT

Vendi Anomaly Scores for Efficient and Accurate Anomaly Detection

Amey Pasarkar, Adji Bousso Dieng

Anomaly detection (AD) is a critical for trustworthy machine learning and scientific discovery. Existing methods typically define anomalies through local density, isolation heuristics, or distance from a fitted distribution, making them sensitive to neighborhood scale, partitioning choices, or restrictive distributional assumptions. In this work, we introduce a new paradigm by formulating anomaly detection from a diversity perspective. We propose the $\textit{Vendi Anomaly Score}$ (VAS), which detects anomalies by quantifying how much a sample changes the diversity of the dataset, as measured by the Vendi score, when removed. VAS captures both local redundancy and global data structure without relying on density estimation or partitioning heuristics. Moreover, VAS requires no hyperparameter tuning, instead adapting automatically to the spectral structure of the data. VAS is non-parametric and scales linearly with dataset size. Across 148 benchmark embedding datasets derived from 10 source datasets and 4 embedding architectures, VAS achieves state-of-the-art AD performance. We further validate VAS on large-scale ImageNet experiments, where it remains robust as both contamination rate and dataset scale increase.

What kills $v$-prediction? A Patch-wise PCA Perspective on Pixel-Space Flow Matching

Tianze Luo, Haofeng Huang, Yuan Yao

Latent Diffusion Models dominate generative modeling but face two-stage training complexity and the inherent reconstruction limits of pre-trained autoencoders. Pixel-space Flow Matching offers a simplified end-to-end alternative yet often encounters training difficulties when using the standard $v$-prediction objective. Recent research attributes this difficulty to the off-manifold nature of noised targets, motivating a shift toward $x$-prediction. In this paper, we investigate this explanation through patch-wise Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and identify a bandwidth bottleneck. The velocity in minor components is largely determined by the raw noisy input, requiring deep backbones to preserve information that competes with principal-component modeling. Through controlled experiments and representation analysis, we further examine why $x$-prediction remains effective: its parameterization provides a raw-input bypass, while its learned input representation can spontaneously concentrate on principal directions. Under low-rank observations, our analysis of the induced flow characterizes $x$-prediction as an implicit conditional-mean decoder, reconstructing discarded components from the retained information. These observations motivate the Raw-injected Transformer (RiT), which combines a lightweight raw-conditioned decoder with an output-side shortcut to separate semantic modeling from raw-state propagation. Our method restores effective $v$-prediction and achieves competitive performance on ImageNet $256\times256$, providing a perspective on the interaction between prediction objectives and architectural bandwidth.

Mitigating Agentic Deception via Evaluation-Side Partially Observable Games

Hanxiao Li, Shutong Liu, Yongjia Ma, Jiaming Ji, Juntao Dai, Shuning Zhang

As large language models become more capable agents, they can exhibit deceptive behaviors, cheating human for higher rewards while appearing helpful. We attribute this failure to evaluation-side partial observability, where models are evaluated mainly from final outcome, without checking the faithfulness of underlying reasoning, tool use, and environmental effects. Prior mitigations address this partial observability by predefined evaluator latent belief space or one-shot monitoring, which do not extend to free-form language space and vulnerable to stronger deceptions that evade simple monitoring. In this work, we formulate deception mitigation as an Evaluation-Side Partially Observable Extensive Form Game (e-PoG), a max-min framework in which an agent maximizes reward, a monitor minimizes the agent's payoff by exposing deception from the full interaction trajectory, and a fixed verifier validates the monitor's claims and converts them into penalties. We define the solution concept as a Subgame-Perfect Oversight Equilibrium (SPOE) between agent and monitor, and prove its existence in finite games. With a perfect verifier, every SPOE is faithful; with an imperfect verifier, the guarantee persists if verifier errors do not reverse the ranking of monitor actions. To achieve faithful SPOE, we derive a two-timescale policy gradient algorithm and enhance the pipeline empirically with NLI verification and deterministic checks. Experiments with Gemma3-4B-IT and Qwen3-4B/8B on Agentic Role-Playing, SearchQA, and WebShop show that e-PoG mitigates multiple emergent deception modes, outperforming the strongest baselines by 26.0% in deception mitigation and by up to 39.8% in task success. These gains persist under weak-to-strong supervision. Code is available at https://github.com/Toyoid/deceptive-agent.

LogCDM: Interpretable Log Anomaly Detection via Behavioral Graph Drift and Adaptive Signal Fusion

Suryansh Talukdar, Taha Sarwat, Jin Soung Yoo

Log-based anomaly detection is increasingly dominated by large pretrained models, from BERT-scale encoders to 8B-parameter language models, which require GPUs at inference time and are difficult to deploy on the monitoring nodes where logs are produced. We present LogCDM, a compact detector that scores each log session using three complementary signals: the next-event surprise of a small causal Transformer, the distance between a graph-attention embedding of the session's own transition graph and that of a normal reference graph, and a transition-drift statistic that we show is exactly a frequency-weighted chi-squared divergence between transition kernels. The divergence view yields an asymptotic null distribution, a length-standardized score, and a per-edge decomposition that explains each alarm. A sigmoid gate fuses the three signals; under a shared-covariance Gaussian model, this gate is the Bayes posterior, and its learned weights indicate which signal each dataset relies on. With 8.1-8.8M parameters and no GPU required at inference, LogCDM reaches F1 scores of 0.984 on HDFS, 0.870 on chronologically split BGL, and 0.850 on Thunderbird, within 0.013-0.12 of an 8.1B-parameter LLM detector evaluated under the same protocol and above all compact baselines on BGL. It scores a session in 5.5-10.5 ms on a CPU. Ablations show that the structural signals add 0.025-0.030 F1 on BGL and Thunderbird but almost nothing on HDFS, consistent with the learned fusion weights.

Learning inexact alternating minimization for convex problems with quadratic coupling

Paul Häusner, Jevgenija Rudzusika, Jens Sjölund, Ozan Öktem

Alternating minimization is a standard tool for optimization problems with two coupled variable blocks. Its cost is dominated by the inner subproblem solvers, which rarely have a closed-form solution. In this paper, we propose to learn an inexact alternating scheme in which neural networks approximate the subproblem solution at each iteration. For convex problems with quadratic couplings between the variable blocks that satisfy a quadratic growth condition, we derive a worst-case bound on the optimality gap that exposes the per-iteration error that each network is trained to minimize in a greedy fashion. Combining the bound with a PAC-Bayes argument, we obtain a fixed-horizon, high-probability bound on the expected optimality gap over the distribution of problem instances. We apply the learned scheme to robust matrix completion and low-dose CT reconstruction with dictionary regularization where we achieve a 5x-13x speedup over the inexact first-order baseline at matched accuracy. A brief end-to-end fine-tuning stage extends this up to 30x and outperforms other learned schemes. The code is available at https://github.com/paulhausner/learned-alternating-minimization.

In our setup, an agent interacting with an unknown environment (modeled as a Markov Decision Process) transmits quantized estimates of a temporal-difference (TD) learning update direction to a server over a finite-capacity channel.
