Structure-Aware Skill Retrieval and Arbitration for Optimization Modeling

Anran Liu, Benzhao Hou, Zongsheng Cao, Daichao Wang, Lang Chen, Shitong Chen

Large Language Models (LLMs) can turn natural-language optimization tasks into solver-checked answers, but current agents remain brittle under narrative rewrites and often store reusable experience at the instance level. TradeWar: A Benchmark for Execution-Grounded Trade Negotiation with Adaptive LLM Agents

Xiangyu Kong, Ruirui Yang, Xia Hou

Multi-turn negotiation remains a challenging problem for large language model (LLM) agents. While LLMs can conduct natural-language dialogue, existing benchmarks predominantly assess only agreement rates, overlooking whether negotiated commitments actually create value once executed. To address this gap, we introduce TradeWar, a multi-country simulation benchmark that translates negotiation dialogues into concrete tariff and trade-share policies, enforces capacity and network constraints during settlement, and evaluates outcomes via an Economic Health Index (EHI). To solve this execution-grounded task, we propose TACTIC (Trade-aware Adaptive Consolidation and Theory-of-mind for International Commerce), an agent that iteratively refines its negotiation strategy across episodes-without updating base LLM weights-by leveraging prior experience, opponent behavior, and past settlement outcomes. Despite using zero-shot chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting, PromptLLM attains a 75% agreement rate but only 57.1 final EHI (*100), whereas TACTIC reaches 68.8. Across 20 sequential episodes and three seeds, TACTIC's final EHI improves from 58.2 to 73.2, compared with 65.2 for an ablation without cross-episode updates. Under technology-controls and energy-crisis shocks, TACTIC also exhibits smaller performance degradation than PromptLLM and MemoryLLM. These results indicate that CoT deliberation and interaction memory alone do not fully address delayed execution feedback, whereas settlement-grounded adaptation improves long-horizon negotiation in TradeWar.

Generative Design and Graph Neural Network Screening of Novel Bimetallic Alloy Catalysts for the Hydrogen Evolution Reaction

Vipin Kurian Elavunkel, Sylvain Le Tonquesse, Hadrien Vroylandt

Identifying earth-abundant catalysts for the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) is a central challenge in scalable green hydrogen production. We present a computational pipeline that couples diffusion-based generative modelling, machine-learning force-field relaxation, and graph neural network (GNN) property prediction to discover bimetallic HER catalysts beyond the structures and stoichiometries covered by existing adsorption databases. A generative model (MatterGen) is fine-tuned on bulk alloy structures labelled with their minimum hydrogen adsorption energy and steered towards adsorption energies near zero. From the generated pool we retain the stable structures that are novel bimetallic alloys: binary compositions built from the training-set metals but at stoichiometries or structures absent from that database. Each candidate's low-index facets are ranked by a machine-learning-force-field (MatterSim) surface-energy screen, and only the most stable terminations are converted into explicit slab models with hydrogen adsorbed at all high-symmetry surface sites and relaxed with the same force field. Two GNN surrogates (MEGNet and ALIGNN), trained on a high-throughput density functional theory (DFT) dataset, then screen the resulting slab candidates. Because the two models encode the local environment through different inductive biases, we retain only the alloys on which both agree, reducing the influence of any single model. This consensus screening, combined with a MatterSim energy-difference triangulation, resulted in a shortlist of near-thermoneutral bimetallic candidates. Eight are validated with plane-wave DFT, and four of them, Pd5Au, Pt5Rh, Cu5Pt3, and Pd13Ru3, bind hydrogen within the thermoneutral window. All eight alloys are dynamically stable by phonon calculations. These four alloys are highlighted as DFT-validated bimetallic HER candidates for experimental follow-up.

Statistical Foundations of Reinforcement Learning: A Non-Asymptotic Perspective

Yuxin Chen, Yuejie Chi, Jianqing Fan, Gen Li, Yuting Wei, Yuling Yan

Reinforcement learning (RL) embodies a foundational paradigm for sequential decision-making in the face of uncertainty. The rapid growth of model complexity in emerging applications has significantly amplified the challenges of achieving data-efficient RL, particularly in sample-starved scenarios where data collection may be costly, time-consuming, or even high-stakes. Consequently, understanding and improving the data efficiency of RL algorithms has become a central problem of both fundamental theoretical interest and practical importance. Despite decades of research, the statistical underpinnings of RL remained remarkably underdeveloped until relatively recently, particularly in the finite-sample regimes most relevant to modern practice. Over the past few years, there has been a surge of activity advancing the frontiers of non-asymptotic statistical RL theory, drawing on ideas from high-dimensional statistics and probability, information theory, and statistical learning theory. In this monograph, we present a non-asymptotic treatment for RL based on tabular Markov decision processes, focusing on three canonical sampling mechanisms --- RL with a generative model, online RL, and offline RL --- together with two algorithmic paradigms: model-based and model-free approaches. Centered around non-asymptotic sample complexity analysis, our treatment emphasizes minimax-optimal algorithm design, particularly methods that attain statistical optimality without significant burn-in costs, as well as sharp statistical characterizations of several widely used RL algorithms. Whenever possible, we provide largely self-contained analyses together with intuitive explanations. We also briefly discuss several extensions, such as robust RL, competitive multi-agent RL, federated RL, uncertainty quantification, and RL from human feedback.

Trainable Vector Quantization for Large Language Models

Anastasiia Prutianova, Alina Bogdanova, Mikhail Shutov, Dmitrii Poimanov, Anatoly Gorkov, Ilia Zharikov

Vector Quantization (VQ) is a rapidly evolving technique for compressing Large Language Model (LLM) weights, reducing memory usage, and speeding up inference. However, extreme compression deteriorates model performance, which is challenging to recover due to the discrete nature of VQ parameters. Existing methods employ training-free approaches that are limited to layer-wise reconstruction loss minimization. To address this limitation, we propose TVQ, a novel method that leverages straight-through estimation to enable gradient descent-based optimization of vector-quantized LLMs. By reformulating VQ LLM optimization with trainable latent weights, TVQ enables arbitrary objective minimization. We constitute our approach by applying TVQ in a block-wise knowledge distillation paradigm, which balances quality and training efficiency, and propose several practical solutions to accelerate training. In experiments on modern LLMs, we demonstrate that TVQ is compatible with modern VQ schemes. As a result, TVQ achieves state-of-the-art quality in low-bit post-training quantization settings with a modest training budget and straightforward PyTorch implementation. For instance, TVQ achieves near-lossless 2-bit quantization for LLaMA2-70B with only 1% accuracy drop on popular Question-Answering benchmarks.

Quantum-mechanical approaches, such as density functional theory (DFT), underpin much of modern computational materials science and are the primary source of data for AI-driven discovery. Its inputs and outputs follow structured, schema-validated data standards, making every result AI-ready. Q3 is open source under Apache 2.0 and built to be reused, extended, and improved by the research community. Q3 points to a paradigm shift in how computational quantum science is done. Scientists define the physics, formulate the models, and set the criteria for validation; AI agents write, test, and optimize the code. The decades-long path from a new theory to a trusted, high-performance implementation can thus collapse to days, significantly accelerating scientific discovery.

Optimizing Stochastic Policy Geometry for Few-Step Flow Reinforcement Learning

Zhuguanyu Wu, Congjia Chen, Ruihao Gong, Yang Yong, Shiqiao Gu, Yi Xu

Few-step flow models generate samples through a handful of large denoising transitions. Applying GRPO to such models requires introducing stochasticity into the denoising transitions, and when a single transition spans a wide range of noise levels, the choice of the resulting stochastic policy becomes a major factor in optimization. Recent constructions constrain the policy to preserve the target flow coefficients, but this constraint admits a family of policies and offers no criterion for choosing among them. We introduce Geo-FSRL (Geometry-Optimized Few-Step Reinforcement Learning), built on a selection principle: at a fixed conditioning state and timestep pair, choose the admissible policy that minimizes the conditional KL divergence induced by any given nonzero velocity-output update. Within a coefficient-preserving family of isotropic Gaussian transitions, this criterion reduces to minimizing a single scalar geometry factor that determines the conditional KL for every velocity-output update. The same factor governs velocity-space Fisher information and conditional transition log-likelihood-ratio variance. We derive the unique closed-form optimizer within this family, shared across all nonzero update magnitudes and directions, with transition coefficients determined by both the source and target noise levels. Because the minimized geometry factor still varies across timesteps, we further adopt gradient-scale matching from prior work and derive closed-form policy-gradient and KL weights that normalize explicit score scales and reference-KL curvature. Geo-FSRL trains on the original few-step schedule and retains the deterministic solver at inference. Our method achieves higher final reward scores than Flow-GRPO and Flow-CPS on both image generation and video generation models under the 4-step setting. Code is available at https://github.com/ModelTC/LightX2V/tree/main/lightx2v_rl

AI evaluators favor job applications written by certain AI models, independent of candidate quality

Nicolò Pagan, Michael Andreas Ebling, Stefania Ionescu, Anikó Hannák

Job applicants increasingly use large language models (LLMs) to write cover letters, while employers deploy LLMs to screen them. In this all-AI setting, we ask whether the \emph{choice of model} affects hiring outcomes independently of candidate qualifications. We introduce a multi-model auditing framework pairing eleven LLM writer agents with nine API-hosted evaluator agents across ten job descriptions and fifty candidates per job. Four findings emerge. First, we identify a generative preference hierarchy: a consistent global ranking of writer models by evaluator acceptance, in which locally hosted models are systematically disadvantaged regardless of their license, compounded by self-preference bias where evaluators favour their own outputs. Second, evaluator models differ substantially in how much bias they introduce based on applicants' tool choice, offering actionable guidance for fairer screening design. Third, this hierarchy has measurable competitive consequences: in the most adverse pairing, approximately two (1.8) out of twenty-five qualified candidates are filtered out purely due to model mismatch. Fourth, a common mechanism explains the patterns above: writer preference and self-preference trace to a combination of how much each writer leans on specific stylistic cues and how strongly evaluators, often unanimously, reward or penalise those same cues. A further check finds little evidence that an unmeasured "writer fingerprint" remains once these same features are accounted for. We discuss implications for algorithmic fairness in employment, regulation of AI-assisted screening, and the design of auditing frameworks that can be reapplied as the model landscape evolves.

Towards Understanding the Impact of Plasticity Loss on Reinforcement Learning in Stochastic Environments

Philipp Bordne, André Biedenkapp

Deep reinforcement learning (RL) is a powerful framework for sequential decision-making. Despite many high-profile successes in recent years, deep RL is known to be brittle. An emerging line of research attributes this brittleness to a loss of plasticity in neural networks. However, plasticity loss has so far been understudied in stochastic environments. We study this interplay systematically, training SAC agents on three DeepMind Control tasks augmented with controlled levels of observation and action noise. We find that plasticity-preserving methods increase learning robustness on the two harder tasks, with their advantage growing as observation noise increases, while combining resets with layer normalization underperforms resets alone at every observation noise level tested. Further, stochasticity can accelerate the loss of plasticity in harder environments, whereas in easier settings noise can act as a regularizer that sustains plasticity. Lastly, plasticity loss is most pronounced in the early stages of training. In the most challenging stochastic settings, TD instability rather than gradual plasticity loss becomes the dominant failure mode.

RoboArchitect: Automating the Design of Robotic Agents with Persistent Memory

Shengguang Wu, Suning Huang, Cherie Ho, Ria Doshi, Hao Zhu, Serena Yeung-Levy

Recent work shows that a frozen robot policy performs far better inside an agent harness, where control code decides when to query the policy, consult vision tools, or command the arm directly. Yet today these harnesses are built by hand and redesigned for each new robot or benchmark. We show that harness design can be automated, offering a strong alternative to finetuning on a novel setup. In RoboArchitect, a coding model observes a robot's rollouts, diagnoses failures, and iteratively revises the harness around the frozen policy. The harness evolves on a set of episodes for its optimization and is evaluated on held-out scenes and tasks. Without updating any model weights, RoboArchitect raises average success from 9.7% to 43.3% on unseen scenes (4.5x) and from 12.7% to 40.2% on unseen tasks (3.2x), across multiple benchmarks and policies. The gains are largest where the benchmark is far from the policy's training data: wrapping a general-purpose policy, the evolved harness yields a specialist that often outperforms even the finetuned version. Intuitive control strategies are automatically designed, such as lifting a jammed arm clear or reaching in horizontally when an obstacle blocks the way from above. The harness also evolves its own persistent memory, carrying learned experience, such as effective grasp depths, into later decisions. These results suggest that substantial headroom in robotic manipulation lies in the agent harness, and its design can be effectively automated and optimized without weight updates.

Regret-Optimal Wasserstein-Robust Linear Regression

Sloan Nietert, Daniel Kuhn

Distributionally robust optimization (DRO) minimizes worst-case risk under distributional uncertainty, but its decisions can be suboptimal in terms of their excess risk, or regret. We study this gap for multivariate least-squares regression, assuming that the observed source law and unknown target law differ by an adversarial 2-Wasserstein perturbation of size $\varepsilon$. We first identify the asymptotic complexity of robust decision-making in the $\varepsilon \to 0$ limit, characterized by a problem-specific condition number $κ$. Here, a wide spectrum of simple algorithms, including DRO, achieve the instance-optimal rate of $O(κ\varepsilon^2)$. For fixed $\varepsilon > 0$, we prove that DRO still achieves the minimax rate if the problem has bounded residual effective rank. On the other hand, we identify significant failure modes where DRO is provably suboptimal by dimension-dependent factors. Fortunately, we prove that two alternative procedures, minimax regret optimization (MRO) and spectral-norm regularization (SNR), attain the minimax rate. We derive an exact convex reformulation of MRO and an accelerated proximal algorithm for SNR, along with high-probability finite-sample guarantees. We support these theoretical results with numerical experiments separating the performance of MRO and SNR from DRO.
